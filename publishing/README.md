# Lifeconomist Publishing and Production Capability

**Status:** Capability documented; initial production format selected; no publication or production schedule committed.  
**Established:** 2026-10-31  
**Canonical location:** This file is the current home for publishing strategy, production-method options, tool roles, and the research-to-publication workflow.  
**Project relationship:** A cross-cutting communication and application capability—not a sixth workstream and not a replacement for the research methodology.

## 1. Purpose

Lifeconomist may communicate selected research through videos, articles, diagrams, visual explanations, threads, and other suitable formats. Publishing can help people understand complex economic and financial systems, build a trusted knowledge brand, demonstrate research capability, create future business opportunities, and potentially generate income.

Publishing must serve the research and its audience. The need to produce content must not dictate what the project investigates, force premature conclusions, or turn the existing research programme into a content calendar.

## 2. Governing principle: research first, publication second, format flexible

Apply the project-wide Continuity, Pause, and Revision Principle in [RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md](../RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md).

- **No production rush:** Do not publish simply to satisfy a schedule or maintain activity.
- **No forced length:** A question may warrant a short explanation, a longer documentary, a series, an article, a diagram, or no publication yet.
- **Format is revisable:** The initial format is a practical starting point, not a permanent commitment.
- **Preserve options without activating everything:** Record useful production methods and possible formats. Keep them available for future consideration without requiring evaluation or use now.
- **Research continuity:** Before selecting material, inspect the relevant source artifacts and current research state. Reuse verified work; do not repeat investigations without a reason.
- **Evidence readiness before production:** A topic need not answer every conceivable question, but its intended claims, scope, source trail, uncertainty, and unresolved issues must be clear enough for the chosen publication.
- **Audience and purpose matter:** Choose a format according to the question, evidence, audience, cost, and explanatory value—not novelty or cinematic appeal alone.
- **No permanent cadence:** Publication timing remains flexible and may change as research progress, resources, audience response, or priorities change.
- **Corrections remain possible:** Published claims may need correction or qualification when material evidence changes. Preserve the correction trail and review dependent publications where appropriate.

## 3. Objectives

### Primary objective — build a trusted knowledge brand

Explain economic and financial systems, institutions, incentives, flows, measurement, and consequences in ways that are understandable without sacrificing accuracy. Trust should come from transparent reasoning, credible sources, honest uncertainty, and willingness to correct errors—not from production polish alone.

### Secondary objective — support future business opportunities

Where the evidence and audience response justify it, publishing may demonstrate capabilities relevant to economic intelligence, research, monitoring, consulting, data products, or other Lifeconomist applications. Do not force every research finding into a commercial pitch.

### Longer-term objective — explore income opportunities

Possible routes include platform revenue, sponsorships, paid research, consulting, subscriptions, and intelligence products or services. These are possibilities to validate, not guaranteed outcomes or reasons to exaggerate claims.

## 4. Initial active publishing path

**Initial format:** Faceless documentary videos with narration, diagrams, evidence-based visualizations, and cinematic or illustrative visuals when they help explain the subject.

**Primary platform:** YouTube first. The initial video is the main authored work; it may be short or long depending on the question and evidence.

**Secondary distribution destinations:** X/Twitter, LinkedIn, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Adapt a suitable publication for each platform when there is a clear reason. Do not assume every item should be posted everywhere, and do not treat cross-posting as automatic.

**Current operating posture:** Flexible and exploratory. There is no fixed publishing frequency, deadline, required first-video topic, production budget, or obligation to start production immediately.

The initial path is intentionally narrow: start with the faceless documentary workflow, learn from practical use, and change the format or distribution approach when evidence or experience gives a reason.

## 5. Production-method register

This is an options register, not a roadmap that must be completed in order. Status describes present use, not the permanent value of a method.

| Method or output | Status | Consider when |
|---|---|---|
| Faceless narrated documentary with diagrams and cinematic/illustrative visuals | **Active starting format** | A researched question benefits from a guided narrative and carefully sequenced explanation. |
| Screen-recorded research or data walkthrough | Available for later | Direct inspection of a source, calculation, chart, dataset, or workflow is central to understanding. |
| Short-form vertical explainer or clip | Available for later | One self-contained insight can be communicated without stripping away essential context. |
| Article, research brief, or written explainer | Available for later | Readers need detail, citations, searchable reference material, or a durable written account. |
| Diagram, chart, or visual essay | Available for later | Relationships, quantities, processes, or comparisons are clearer visually than through narration alone. |
| Presenter-led video | Available for later | A human presenter materially improves explanation, trust, or audience connection. |
| Interview or expert conversation | Available for later | Relevant expertise or first-hand experience is accessible and the interview can be prepared and verified. |
| Animation or data story | Available for later | Motion or a designed visual sequence clarifies a mechanism that would otherwise be hard to follow. |
| Audio-first explanation or podcast | Available for later | The material works without visual evidence, or audio is the most practical format. |
| Other future methods | Unselected | A specific question, audience need, opportunity, or production constraint makes them worth considering. |

Do not build all these production capabilities in advance. Change a method's status only when there is a practical reason, and record material changes in the project history.

## 6. Research-to-publication workflow

Use these stages proportionately. They are quality gates and a reusable guide, not a mandatory linear production pipeline or a promise to publish every research output.

1. **Select a question:** Choose a bounded, meaningful question from existing research or a new investigation. Check CURRENT-STATE.md, the relevant research-log entries, decisions, and source artifacts. Identify the intended audience and why the explanation may be useful.
2. **Check research readiness:** Identify what is established, interpreted, hypothesized, disputed, or unknown. Verify important sources and calculations at the level needed for the claims. If essential evidence is missing, continue research, narrow the claim, or pause publication.
3. **Choose the explanatory angle:** State the central question, what the audience should understand, the mechanism or evidence to show, and what the work will not claim. Avoid compressing several large questions into one video merely because they are connected.
4. **Choose the format and scope:** Start from the active faceless documentary format, but reconsider it if another format would serve the material better. Let the question determine length and complexity.
5. **Prepare a source-backed script and visual plan:** Link factual claims to sources; plan diagrams, charts, maps, examples, and illustrative footage. Clearly distinguish real evidence from hypothetical examples, reconstruction, simulation, and AI-generated illustrative material.
6. **Review before production:** Check accuracy, source context, dates, units, causal language, uncertainty, alternative explanations, relevant counter-evidence, and whether the narrative overstates what the evidence establishes. Simplify wording without simplifying away the mechanism.
7. **Produce and review the cut:** Record narration, create or source visuals responsibly, edit, and review the complete work for factual consistency, intelligibility, accessibility, rights, privacy, and unintended misleading impressions.
8. **Publish when ready:** Choose title, description, citations/source notes, and release timing. Do not impose a deadline when the work is not ready or the cost is not justified.
9. **Adapt selectively:** Derive platform-specific clips, posts, threads, or written versions only where useful. Preserve necessary context and link to the fuller explanation when appropriate.
10. **Record and learn:** Save the final script, source list, asset/licence notes, publication link, date, format, corrections, and useful audience feedback. Treat views and engagement as evidence about distribution and audience response, not proof that a factual claim is true or that the work created business value.

A project may stop at any stage. Research may be retained without publication; a script may be paused; a completed video may be published only on one platform; and an existing publication may be revised or withdrawn if a material problem warrants it.

## 7. Evidence, explanation, and integrity standards

All published work inherits the standards in [RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md](../RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md) and relevant source artifacts.

- Separate verified facts, supported interpretations, hypotheses, illustrative scenarios, and unresolved questions.
- Cite or clearly identify material sources in a way viewers can find and assess them.
- Check the original source where the claim requires it; a headline, search snippet, abstract, or second-hand summary may not be sufficient.
- Do not imply causation from correlation, sequence, incentives, or a plausible mechanism alone.
- Preserve units, time periods, population boundaries, definitions, and other context that materially affects a claim.
- Do not use dramatic narration, selective visuals, music, or editing to imply a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports.
- Label hypothetical numbers and illustrative scenarios clearly. Never present generated visuals as documentary evidence of a real event.
- Use AI tools as assistants for drafting, synthesis, or visuals, not as independent authorities. Verify factual claims against reliable source material.
- Respect copyright, licences, privacy, consent, and platform rules. Keep a record of asset origins and relevant permissions.
- Correct significant mistakes openly and assess whether derivative clips or posts also need correction.

Every chain or arrow diagram should be followed by a plain-language explanation of what the mechanism means. Visual compression must not replace accessible explanation.

## 8. Tool roles and adoption policy

Tools should reduce a demonstrated bottleneck or improve traceability, quality, or production feasibility. Their mention here does not mean every tool is installed, integrated, approved, or required. Check current pricing, plan limits, access, licensing, and terms before depending on a service.

| Tool or environment | Intended role | Current policy |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub repository | Canonical project decisions, research records, methodology, and publication strategy | **Authoritative project record.** Important findings and decisions return here. |
| Zotero | Bibliographic records, source metadata, papers, reports, and citation management | Candidate source/reference manager; adopt or deepen use when source tracking benefits. |
| Obsidian | Connected exploratory notes and personal knowledge navigation using Markdown | Optional working layer. It does not replace the canonical GitHub record; transfer important conclusions and decisions back. |
| Google NotebookLM | Temporary source-grounded synthesis, comparison, and question exploration over selected materials | Optional assistant. Check its outputs against original sources; do not treat notebooks or generated summaries as canonical evidence. |
| OBS Studio | Screen capture and/or recording narration | Candidate production tool, especially for recording a research walkthrough or source demonstration. |
| DaVinci Resolve or another suitable editor | Assemble narration, diagrams, footage, sound, captions, and final exports | Choose based on actual hardware, learning cost, and production needs; no editor is mandated. |
| Higgsfield or other visual-generation tools | Generate selected illustrative or cinematic visuals where they add explanatory value | Optional and selective. Not a substitute for sourced evidence; review cost, usage rights, output quality, and disclosure needs. |
| LibreOffice Calc / Google Sheets | Small calculations, data checks, simple charts, and production tracking | Use when it materially helps; retain formulas, definitions, and sources for analytical claims. |
| Python and diagramming tools | Reproducible calculations, custom charts, and clear mechanism diagrams | Use only when the task benefits; do not add technical complexity for its own sake. |
| Audio tools | Clean narration and prepare intelligible sound | Choose only if audio quality becomes a practical need. |

**Low-cost default:** Reuse the repository, available free or already-accessible tools, and simple production techniques first. Do not subscribe to multiple tools or buy expensive cinematic assets before a specific need has been demonstrated. Check actual hardware performance before committing to demanding editing or rendering workflows.

### Source-of-truth boundaries

- **GitHub:** canonical project decisions, approved methodology, research state, scripts when ready for durable review, and publication records.
- **Zotero:** bibliographic metadata and reference-library management; a citation entry does not by itself establish that a source supports a claim.
- **Obsidian:** optional exploratory and linked working notes.
- **NotebookLM:** temporary source-grounded workspace and synthesis aid.
- **Production files:** scripts, storyboards, narration, raw footage, generated assets, project files, captions, exports, and licence records. Keep them in an appropriately backed-up working location and link or summarize durable project-relevant records in GitHub.

Avoid unnecessary duplication. Preserve links and provenance, and move substantive findings or decisions to the canonical project records.

## 9. Publication record

When a specific item enters active development, create a record in publishing/productions/ using a descriptive filename. Do not create empty records for every possible future idea.

A production record should capture, as relevant:

- Working title and unique ID.
- Status: idea, research check, scripting, production, review, ready, published, paused, cancelled, or correction required.
- Core question, audience, purpose, scope, and format.
- Links to the existing Lifeconomist research artifacts and decision records used.
- Claims requiring verification, source trail, key definitions, uncertainty, and excluded claims.
- Script and storyboard versions; original versus illustrative/generated visuals.
- Asset sources, licences, permissions, and attribution.
- Review checklist, publication destinations and links, release date, adaptations, corrections, and lessons.
- The next useful action and reason for pausing or changing direction, when applicable.

The record preserves continuity; it should not become a bureaucratic barrier to a small, low-risk publication.

## 10. Revision and learning

Review the approach when real experience supplies a reason—for example, audience comprehension, production time, cost, accessibility, platform performance, a new distribution opportunity, or an evidence/correction issue. Separate factual corrections from changes to style, scope, platform, or business strategy.

Do not infer content quality from views alone, and do not infer business impact from engagement alone. If an important change affects the current strategy or project assumptions, update this record and the relevant project-level records with the reason.

# Test Video Production Plan — When a Bank Makes a Loan, Where Does the Money Come From?

**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**Status:** Practical first-cut plan. Production can begin; this plan is adjustable and does not lock the final runtime, tools, style, or publication decision.  
**Script:** [Script Draft v0.1](script-draft-bank-loan-deposit-creation-v0.1.md)  
**Outline:** [Preliminary Explainer Outline](outline-bank-loan-deposit-creation.md)  
**Source audit:** [Source Audit — Commercial-Bank Loan and Deposit Creation](source-audit-bank-loan-deposit-creation.md)

## Goal

Make one complete test video from the existing script. Learn the actual workflow—narration, visuals, editing, sound, and review—rather than trying to perfect every decision in advance.

## Production approach

- Format: faceless explainer with documentary-style narration, simple diagrams, and restrained supporting visuals.
- Priority: understandable narration and accurate diagrams. Cinematic footage is optional support, not the main explanation.
- First-cut rule: use simple assets that are available now. Replace or improve them only when the first edit shows a real problem.
- Cost rule: do not buy subscriptions or assets just to complete this experiment.
- Editorial rule: production choices are provisional. The completed video can be revised, shortened, rerecorded, or abandoned after review.

## Scene-by-scene shot list

| Scene | Narration section | What viewers see | Production notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The question | “When you borrow money from a bank…” through “Let's follow the entries.” | Brief everyday banking image, then the title question on a clean background. | Keep the opening short. Avoid dramatic vaults, conspiratorial imagery, or generic footage that delays the question. |
| 2. Follow the entries | The hypothetical $100 loan and the matching bank/borrower entries. | Two clear panels: **The bank** and **The borrower**. Reveal each entry in sequence, then hold the complete diagram on screen. | This is the key visual. Use $100 consistently. Clearly mark the example as hypothetical. Use readable type and high contrast. |
| 3. What this does—and does not—mean | New deposit, new debt, and lending constraints. | Reuse the same diagram. Highlight “deposit created” and “debt incurred.” Then show a short list: repayment risk, costs, capital and rules, payments/liquidity, demand and economic conditions. | Do not introduce every technical detail. The visual should reinforce the narration, not become a dense checklist. |
| 4. Money is not the same as wealth | What happens after the loan is used. | A few ordinary real-world examples—such as a shop transaction or equipment repair—then a simple contrast: **Financial claim** vs **real-world outcome**. | Use available footage, still images, or simple illustrations. Examples are illustrative, not evidence that every loan produces value. |
| 5. The answer | Closing narration. | Return to the bank/borrower diagram. Finish with “Deposit created” alongside “Debt incurred,” then display the central question or a concise closing line. | End cleanly. Avoid adding a new topic or a long subscribe message that distracts from the answer. |

## The balance-sheet graphic

Use this as the source of truth for the central visual:

**The bank**

| Entry | Change |
|---|---:|
| Loan owed by borrower — asset | +$100 |
| Deposit owed to customer — liability | +$100 |

**The borrower**

| Entry | Change |
|---|---:|
| Bank deposit — asset | +$100 |
| Loan owed to bank — liability | +$100 |

The diagram means that the bank has a claim on the borrower and also owes the borrower a deposit balance. The borrower has an asset they can use to make payments and a debt they must repay. The entries describe the transaction; they do not mean the borrower received a debt-free gift or that real resources appeared automatically.

Small on-screen note: **“Hypothetical example; $100 is used for illustration only.”**

## Narration and audio

1. Record the existing script in short scene-sized sections rather than as one long take.
2. Use a quiet room and the best microphone already available. A phone or laptop microphone is acceptable for the test if the recording is clear.
3. Record a short test first and listen back for noise, clipping, echo, and understandable pacing.
4. Speak naturally. Pause briefly when a new diagram entry appears so the viewer can read it.
5. If a sentence is difficult to say naturally, mark it for a possible edit rather than stopping the entire workflow.
6. Keep background music optional and very quiet. Clear narration matters more than music.

## Editing order

1. Put the narration on the timeline in scene order.
2. Remove long gaps and obvious recording mistakes; do not over-polish breaths or every pause.
3. Add the title and the balance-sheet graphic.
4. Add only the supporting visuals needed to keep the explanation engaging.
5. Add simple transitions and restrained background audio only if they improve the result.
6. Watch the complete video once without stopping. Write down the three most noticeable problems.
7. Fix those problems, export a review copy, and watch it again.

## Minimum review checklist

- [ ] Can a non-specialist understand the main question within the opening?
- [ ] Does the $100 example remain consistent across narration and graphics?
- [ ] Can viewers distinguish the bank's asset from its liability, and the borrower's asset from their debt?
- [ ] Does the narration explain the diagram in plain language?
- [ ] Are the words readable on a phone screen?
- [ ] Is narration easy to hear and understand?
- [ ] Do visuals support the explanation rather than imply fraud or unlimited money creation?
- [ ] Are the source links preserved so factual claims can be checked?
- [ ] Is there any clear factual error or misleading statement that should be corrected before sharing?

Do not use this checklist to delay the first cut over minor stylistic issues. Fix obvious factual errors and major comprehension problems; note smaller improvements for the next version.

## First-cut completion standard

The test video is ready for review when it has:
- narration for all five scenes;
- the complete, legible balance-sheet diagram;
- enough supporting visuals to avoid a static slideshow throughout;
- intelligible audio;
- a complete beginning, middle, and end; and
- a saved review export.

It does **not** need a permanent brand package, custom animation, paid footage, perfect color grading, a fixed runtime, or a publication date.

## After the first cut

Review the actual video, not just the plan. Decide whether to:
- make a few targeted edits;
- rerecord selected narration;
- simplify or replace a confusing visual;
- export a revised version;
- share it privately for feedback; or
- pause publication while preserving what was learned.

No public release is implied by completing the test video. The purpose of this first pass is to learn the workflow and discover what needs improving.

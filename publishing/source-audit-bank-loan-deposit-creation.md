## 1. Question being tested

When a commercial bank grants a loan and credits the borrower's deposit account, does it simply transfer deposits previously saved by someone else, or does the transaction create a new bank deposit?

**Preliminary answer supported by the sources reviewed:** In the standard modern commercial-banking case, granting a loan normally creates a matching bank deposit at origination. This is creation of deposit money, not creation of real resources or an automatic increase in the borrower's net wealth. The bank receives a loan claim (asset) and owes the customer the deposit (liability); the borrower receives a deposit asset and incurs a loan liability. The explanation must distinguish aggregate banking-system deposit creation from the subsequent funding and settlement needs of an individual bank.

## 2. Source register

### S1 — Bank of England, “Money creation in the modern economy” (2014)

- **URL:** https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy
- **Authors:** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia, and Ryland Thomas.
- **Source type:** Central-bank research article.
- **Relevant locations:** Overview; “Two misconceptions about money creation”; “Money creation in reality”; “Other ways of creating and destroying deposits”; “Limits to broad money creation.”
- **What it supports:** Commercial-bank lending creates a matching deposit; deposits are bank liabilities; loan repayment destroys deposit money in the standard case; central-bank reserves are distinct from customer deposits; banks face profitability, borrower-demand, risk, regulatory, settlement/funding, and monetary-policy constraints.
- **Scope limitation:** This article explains the UK monetary system and uses UK institutional examples. Use it for the general balance-sheet mechanism with appropriate qualifications; do not transplant UK statistics, regulations, reserve arrangements, or current policy details to Nigeria.
- **Audit note:** The article itself says that simplified loan and deposit amounts may diverge over time because other transactions also create or destroy deposits. Avoid presenting the initial one-for-one balance-sheet illustration as a complete model of subsequent money-stock changes.

### S2 — Bank of England, “How is money created?” (updated 1 October 2019)

- **URL:** https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/explainers/how-is-money-created
- **Source type:** Central-bank public explainer.
- **Relevant locations:** “How does it work?” and “Can banks create as much money as they like?”
- **What it supports:** Accessible explanation of deposit creation when a bank credits a borrower; the difference between money creation and wealth creation; loan repayment and deposit reduction; the fact that bank lending is constrained.
- **Scope limitation:** The quantitative composition of UK money and references to Bank of England accounts are UK-specific. Do not reuse its percentages as current or Nigerian figures.
- **Audit note:** Useful for plain-language framing; use the detailed 2014 article for more precise mechanisms and caveats.

### S3 — International Monetary Fund, Marco Gross and Christoph Siebenbrunner, “Money Creation in Fiat and Digital Currency Systems” (IMF Working Paper 19/285, 2019)

- **URL:** https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2019/12/20/money-creation-in-fiat-and-digital-currency-systems-48843
- **Source type:** IMF working paper; authors' views, not necessarily those of the IMF, its Executive Board, or management.
- **Relevant location:** Published summary and paper definitions/discussion.
- **What it supports:** The distinction between commercial-bank lending, which creates a deposit in the authors' framework, and lending by centralized nonbank financial institutions or through decentralized bond-market intermediation, which transfers existing money rather than creating aggregate money in the same way; the role of reserve transfers and monetary policy in a multi-bank system.
- **Scope limitation:** This is a technical working paper with a defined model and terminology. It should complement, not replace, the simpler central-bank explanation. Avoid presenting its framing as the only theoretical account of every institution or transaction.

### S4 — Bank for International Settlements, “Fundamentals of money: its origin, concept and operation in the modern economy” (speech, 5 August 2025)

- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/speeches/20250805-fundamentals-money-its-origin-concept-and-operation-modern-economy
- **Source type:** BIS speech/explanatory material.
- **What it supports:** Corroborates that deposits are generally created when banks make loans, and distinguishes deposit creation from reserve transfers and the lending activities of nonbank institutions.
- **Scope limitation:** A speech is not a substitute for a detailed technical paper. The page was discoverable in search, but full-page retrieval was not successful during this pass. Treat this as corroboration only until the full text is reviewed directly.

## 4. Visual mechanism to test

**Simplified origination example — hypothetical units, not Nigerian data**

Before lending, the diagram is not meant to show a whole bank's complete balance sheet; it isolates the entries relevant to this transaction.

1. A commercial bank approves a loan of 100 units and credits the borrower's deposit account.
2. **Bank:** loan asset +100; customer-deposit liability +100.
3. **Borrower:** deposit asset +100; loan liability +100.

The entries balance. The borrower now has spendable deposit money and an equal new debt obligation. The bank has a claim on the borrower and a matching obligation to the borrower. This transaction alone does not create a new house, machine, medicine, food, or other real resource; it creates financial claims and a means of payment. The later use of the loan may finance purchases or investment, but the outcomes depend on the real transaction and its consequences.

A second visual should show what happens after the borrower pays a seller at another bank: the deposit may move to the seller's bank, while interbank settlement creates liquidity/funding implications for the lending bank. This is important to prevent the false inference that a bank can create deposits without managing payments, risk, and funding.

A third, optional visual can show principal repayment reducing the relevant deposit money in the standard bank-loan case. Do not overload the first explanation with every possible transaction.

## 6. Preliminary conclusion

The core, narrowly framed question is supported by multiple relevant institutional sources, especially the detailed Bank of England article and the IMF working paper's distinction between bank and nonbank lending. The candidate is promising enough to proceed to a script outline **only after** we decide that the source scope and conceptual caveats are sufficient for the intended public explanation. A full script should still be checked claim by claim, and Nigeria-specific claims should remain excluded unless separately verified.

The governing idea is not “banks can create unlimited money.” It is: commercial-bank loan origination can create a matching deposit, but this accounting mechanism operates inside a wider system of repayment, payments, funding, risk, regulation, borrower demand, and monetary policy.

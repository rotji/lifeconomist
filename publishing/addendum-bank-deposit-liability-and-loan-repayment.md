# Explainer Addendum — What the Bank Owes the Borrower, and What Happens When the Loan Is Repaid

**Date:** 2026-11-04  
**Status:** Conceptual clarification added after a viewer-comprehension question. Use this as an extension to the first bank-loan/deposit-creation explainer, not as a replacement for the original source audit.

## Why this clarification is needed

The existing script explains that a newly credited deposit is a liability of the bank and an asset of the borrower. That accounting statement is correct, but an everyday bank customer may reasonably ask: if the bank owes me the deposit, does it have to pay me the same way I have to repay my loan?

The answer needs to distinguish two different obligations.

## The two obligations are not the same

In the hypothetical $100 loan example:

**The borrower owes the bank $100 principal.** This is a loan obligation. The borrower must repay it according to the loan agreement.

**The bank owes the borrower a $100 deposit balance.** This is a deposit liability. The bank must honour the balance under the account's terms and applicable payment arrangements. The customer can ordinarily use the deposit to make payments or transfer it, or withdraw cash subject to the account terms, available services, and applicable rules.

The bank does not normally make a second, separate $100 payment to the borrower simply because the deposit is recorded as its liability. The $100 credited to the account is already the customer's deposit claim. When the customer withdraws or transfers it, the bank honours that claim; it is not repaying the loan principal on the customer's behalf.

## What happens when principal is repaid?

In the standard simplified bank-loan case, when the borrower repays $100 of loan principal from a bank deposit:

- the borrower's deposit balance falls by $100;
- the borrower's loan liability falls by $100;
- the bank's deposit liability falls by $100; and
- the bank's loan asset falls by $100.

In this simplified case, the deposit money used for principal repayment is extinguished as the loan asset is reduced. Do not imply that interest is treated identically: interest is a separate payment/income item, and this explainer should focus on principal only.

## Plain-language analogy

The bank has two different relationships with the borrower at once: it has lent the borrower money, so the borrower owes the bank; and it has credited an account, so the bank must honour the customer's deposit balance. The customer can spend or withdraw the deposit, but still remains responsible for repaying the loan under the loan agreement.

## Accuracy and scope guardrails

- Keep the example hypothetical and use $100 consistently.
- Say “deposit balance” or “bank deposit,” not physical cash sitting in a box.
- Do not suggest the bank owes a second $100 on top of the credited deposit.
- Do not suggest a customer withdrawal is the same transaction as repaying the loan.
- Explain that a transfer or withdrawal uses/settles the customer's deposit claim; loan principal repayment reduces the loan and, in the standard case, the corresponding deposit money.
- Keep the explanation at the level of the standard simplified case. Actual account access, withdrawal conditions, settlement processes, and loan terms depend on contract and jurisdiction.
- Do not introduce Nigeria-specific legal or regulatory claims without current Nigerian primary sources.

# First Publication Candidate Review

**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**Status:** Preliminary screening plus initial source audit; candidate remains provisional, with no production commitment or final topic lock.  
**Purpose:** Identify a bounded, worthwhile public-explanation question from existing Lifeconomist research without repeating investigations or treating the new publishing capability as a reason to produce content.

## 1. Screening method

A candidate should:
- be expressible as one clear question;
- connect to existing project research rather than require rebuilding a large topic from scratch;
- have a credible source trail and a mechanism that can be explained visually;
- matter to a general audience without requiring extensive specialist background;
- allow claims and exclusions to be stated clearly;
- be small enough to verify with modest resources;
- avoid unresolved measurement or evidence dependencies that would make the public explanation misleading.

A strong concept note is not automatically publication-ready. The original sources must be checked against the specific claims selected for the eventual script.

## 2. Preliminary shortlist

### Candidate A — How does a commercial-bank loan create a deposit?

**Proposed bounded question:** When a commercial bank grants a loan and credits the borrower's account, does it simply hand over deposits already saved by someone else, or does the lending transaction create a new bank deposit?

**Existing foundation:** [Concept 10 — Credit, Debt, Banking and Financial Intermediation](../workstream-a/concepts/credit-debt-banking-and-financial-intermediation.md), especially its treatment of bank balance sheets, deposit creation, reserves and settlement, repayment, and constraints on lending. It cites the Bank of England's *Money creation in the modern economy*, the BIS, the IMF, and the Central Bank of Nigeria as sources for further verification.

**Why it is a promising first candidate**
- One clear question with a common misconception to investigate.
- A simple, visual balance-sheet sequence can explain the mechanism without requiring a large documentary.
- The existing concept note already contains the core distinctions and explicit caveats.
- It can be explained internationally while clearly separating general banking mechanics from Nigerian institutional specifics.
- It provides a natural example of Lifeconomist's method: explain the real-world transaction, trace the linked assets and liabilities, then identify constraints and limits.

**Proposed scope**
1. Start with a simplified loan in which a commercial bank credits a borrower's deposit account.
2. Show the corresponding loan asset and deposit liability on the bank's balance sheet and the deposit asset and debt liability for the borrower.
3. Explain why this is not the same as saying banks can lend without limit.
4. Briefly distinguish commercial-bank customer deposits from central-bank reserves.
5. State that a loan creates an obligation and does not automatically create net wealth, productive investment, or improved welfare.

**Explicit exclusions for a first explanation**
- Do not attempt to explain the entire monetary system, central-bank operating framework, or every payment rail.
- Do not claim every form of lending creates new deposit money; lending by non-bank institutions can work differently.
- Do not present the money multiplier as a universal mechanical description.
- Do not imply deposit creation removes constraints from creditworthy demand, risk, capital, liquidity, settlement, profitability, regulation, or borrower repayment.
- Do not make Nigeria-specific claims about current aggregates or regulatory details without checking current CBN sources.

**Readiness:** Strong conceptual starting point. The initial core-mechanism source audit is now documented in [Source Audit — Commercial-Bank Loan and Deposit Creation](source-audit-bank-loan-deposit-creation.md). Core general claims are supported by the Bank of England and IMF materials reviewed; Nigeria-specific institutional claims remain unaudited and excluded by default. A claim-by-claim script review would still be required before any script is approved.

### Candidate B — Is money the same thing as wealth?

**Proposed bounded question:** Why can a household, business, or country have valuable assets but still lack money available to pay obligations—and why is money supply not the same as total wealth?

**Existing foundation:** [Concept 9 — Money, Wealth, Capital and Financial Resources](../workstream-a/concepts/money-wealth-capital-and-financial-resources.md) and [Concept 32 — Commonly Confused Economic and Financial Terms](../workstream-a/concepts/commonly-confused-economic-and-financial-terms-comparison-register.md).

**Strengths:** Broad relevance; useful everyday examples; connects money, assets, liabilities, income, liquidity and solvency.

**Risks:** The question can become too broad by mixing household liquidity, national wealth, monetary aggregates, financial claims and balance-sheet consolidation. It would need a narrower audience and carefully chosen example to avoid turning into a glossary.

**Readiness:** Strong conceptual foundation; likely suitable after selecting one central example and checking the relevant accounting/statistical definitions.

### Candidate C — Does GDP growth automatically mean people are better off?

**Proposed bounded question:** What can real GDP growth tell us about an economy, and what can it not establish about household living standards or development?

**Existing foundation:** [Concept 26 — Economic Growth, Development and Structural Transformation](../workstream-a/concepts/economic-growth-development-and-structural-transformation.md), [Concept 13 — National Income, GDP and Economic Accounting](../workstream-a/concepts/national-income-gdp-and-economic-accounting.md), and the project's four-layer framework.

**Strengths:** High public relevance and a direct connection to the economic-state model.

**Risks:** It can invite overly broad claims about distribution, inflation, population, productivity, public services and welfare. Nigeria-specific headline data should not be used casually: the [Real GDP Growth evidence audit](../03-measurement/01-real-gdp-growth-evidence-audit.md) records unresolved source-vintage and comparability issues, and the NBS workbook itself had not been inspected at the last checkpoint.

**Readiness:** Suitable for a later carefully bounded explainer, but not the strongest low-friction first candidate if the aim is to avoid unresolved measurement dependencies.

## 3. Preliminary recommendation

**Advance Candidate A to source verification, not yet to production.**

Working title for research purposes only: **“When a Bank Makes a Loan, Where Does the Money Come From?”**

The title is a provisional framing, not a claim that the answer is simple or that all types of lending work identically. The more precise central question is whether a commercial bank creates a deposit when it makes a loan, and what balance-sheet entries and constraints explain that process.

Candidate A currently offers the best balance of bounded scope, a clear visual mechanism, existing conceptual work, and a manageable verification task. This is a provisional judgment based on the repository review, not a final audience-tested topic decision.

## 4. Verification completed and remaining

The initial source audit is documented in [Source Audit — Commercial-Bank Loan and Deposit Creation](source-audit-bank-loan-deposit-creation.md). The Bank of England's 2014 article was read directly, including its sections on deposit creation, repayment, constraints, and settlement. The Bank of England public explainer and IMF Working Paper 19/285 were also reviewed. A BIS speech was identified as corroboration, but full-text retrieval was not successful in this pass.

Before scripting or approving a script:
1. Confirm the intended audience and choose the desired scope: a concise general explanation or a slightly broader account including payment settlement.
2. Use the source-audit matrix to verify every factual script claim and preserve the relevant caveat.
3. Keep Nigeria-specific institutional claims out unless current CBN primary sources are separately checked.
4. Check the accounting example independently and label it as a simplified illustration.
5. Review the planned visual sequence in plain language, including what it does not demonstrate.
6. Reconsider whether the candidate remains worthwhile; source support does not obligate us to publish.

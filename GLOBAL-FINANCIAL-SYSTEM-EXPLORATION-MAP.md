# Global Financial System Exploration Map

**Status:** Broad exploratory programme; taxonomy and research agenda are provisional. No global aggregate, dataset, causal model, or opportunity has been validated by this document.  
**Started:** 2026-10-28  
**Purpose:** Explore how wealth, money, credit, capital, financial claims, institutions, countries, households, businesses, governments, markets, and real economic activity fit together. Preserve multiple possible directions until evidence and practical work justify prioritization.

## 1. Governing intention

The aim is to build a broad, evidence-led understanding of the global financial system before specializing. Do not assume at the outset that the primary objective is only to measure wealth, money supply, liquidity, investable capital, government resources, market opportunities, or inequality. These are distinct but related questions that should remain available for exploration.

Start by mapping the landscape, identifying what can be measured, investigating relationships and limitations, and learning from real-world data. Let evidence, data quality, explanatory value, feasibility, and practical relevance inform later priorities. Broad exploration is structured discovery, not indiscriminate collection.

This programme uses the shared principles in [RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md](RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md), including Reality-First, interconnected reality, layered investigation, triangulation, alternative explanations, and explicit uncertainty.

## 2. Relationship to the existing Lifeconomist architecture

This document is a cross-workstream exploration map, **not a sixth workstream, a replacement economic model, or an expansion of the six core indicators by default**.

- **A — Deep Conceptual Foundations:** Clarify what money, wealth, credit, liquidity, capital, ownership, control, debt, financial claims, and related concepts mean in practice.
- **B — Measurement:** Define and validate individual measurements, units, formulas, sector boundaries, valuation methods, frequencies, vintages, revisions, coverage, and limitations.
- **C — Evidence-Based Monitoring:** Track observations and changes, compare sources and periods, and investigate possible implications without prematurely classifying the economy.
- **D — Footprint Intelligence:** Investigate observable evidence, entities, ownership and other relationships, transactions, dependencies, mechanisms, and cross-domain transmission.
- **E — Opportunity Intelligence and Economic Application:** Explore possible implications for decisions, policy understanding, trading, investing, businesses, and intelligence services, without assuming every finding must yield an opportunity.

The existing Economic State Transition Model retains its six foundational indicator candidates and four-layer analytical framework. This wider financial exploration may provide concepts, supporting measurements, or evidence about mechanisms; it does not automatically make every financial measure a core indicator. Continue the existing Workstream B measurement workflow unless research evidence justifies a documented change.

## 3. Initial exploration taxonomy

The taxonomy is open-ended and may overlap. A single instrument or institution can belong to several views at once.

### 3.1 Wealth, assets, and ownership
- Household wealth, distribution, concentration, and balance sheets.
- Business assets, equity, retained earnings, and liabilities.
- Public assets, public liabilities, and government net worth.
- Real assets: land, housing, infrastructure, equipment, inventories, and other productive or non-productive assets.
- Financial assets and claims, including deposits, securities, loans, shares, fund units, derivatives, and pension entitlements.
- Legal ownership, beneficial ownership where observable, custodianship, delegated management, and effective control.
- Valuation changes, asset-price effects, realized transactions, and differences between gross and net measures.

### 3.2 Money, deposits, reserves, and liquidity
- Currency in circulation and monetary aggregates.
- Commercial-bank deposits and central-bank reserves.
- Central-bank balance sheets, facilities, and monetary operations.
- Payment and settlement balances.
- Liquidity by institution, currency, instrument, maturity, and accessibility.
- Distinctions between cash, bank money, near-money, market liquidity, funding liquidity, and solvency.
- Money creation, lending, repayment, asset purchases, and other balance-sheet changes.

### 3.3 Credit, debt, and leverage
- Household, business, financial-sector, and government credit.
- Bank loans, non-bank lending, private credit, trade credit, bonds, and other debt instruments.
- Public debt by holder, currency, maturity, interest structure, and governing terms where data allow.
- Debt-service burden, refinancing needs, arrears, defaults, and restructuring.
- Collateral, margin, secured funding, guarantees, contingent liabilities, and off-balance-sheet exposures.
- Credit standards, credit availability, lending demand, and credit allocation.
- Interconnected exposures between lenders, borrowers, guarantors, and asset holders.

### 3.4 Capital pools and institutional investors
- Pension funds, insurers, mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, asset managers, sovereign wealth funds, central-bank reserve portfolios, endowments, foundations, family offices, hedge funds, venture capital, private equity, private credit, and development finance.
- Assets under management versus cash, commitments, uncalled capital, deployed capital, and realizable proceeds.
- Mandates, liquidity requirements, investment horizons, risk limits, regulation, redemption terms, and other constraints.
- Fundraising, deployment, distributions, exits, valuation changes, and reallocation.
- Differences between announced funding, commitments, signed transactions, disbursements, and realized investments.

### 3.5 Governments and public finance
- Tax revenue, non-tax revenue, expenditure, transfers, subsidies, public investment, and public procurement.
- Fiscal deficits and surpluses, financing needs, debt service, cash balances, and arrears.
- Public enterprises, public assets, guarantees, and contingent liabilities.
- Domestic and foreign creditors, official lending, grants, and development finance.
- Budget execution compared with announcements and appropriations.
- Distributional effects, fiscal capacity, policy trade-offs, and alternative uses of public resources.
- Fiscal flows must be distinguished from asset stocks and from counterfactual savings.

### 3.6 Countries, currencies, and cross-border finance
- International investment positions and balance of payments.
- Foreign-exchange reserves, currency composition, external debt, and external assets.
- Cross-border banking claims and liabilities, direct investment, portfolio investment, remittances, trade finance, and official transfers.
- Exchange rates, currency mismatches, capital controls, conversion access, settlement constraints, and currency-specific funding conditions.
- Global and regional financial centres, correspondent banking, custody, clearing, and settlement relationships.
- Repatriation, capital flight, offshore holdings, and financial secrecy only where defined evidence supports the inquiry.
- Differences between residence-based, nationality-based, ownership-based, and custody-based statistics.

### 3.7 Financial markets, prices, and instruments
- Government and corporate bonds, equities, foreign exchange, money markets, commodities, property, securitized products, derivatives, and other instruments.
- Prices, yields, spreads, bid/ask quotes, volumes, volatility, open interest, and market depth where measurable.
- Risk premia, duration, maturity transformation, collateral quality, haircuts, and margin requirements.
- Market capitalization versus investable value, turnover versus net capital flow, and price changes versus transactions.
- Issuance, refinancing, secondary-market trading, and the difference between trading in existing claims and financing new activity.

### 3.8 Businesses, production, households, and the real economy
- Corporate cash, working capital, inventories, trade receivables and payables, borrowing, investment, and cash flow.
- Household income, consumption, saving, deposits, housing, pensions, borrowing, and net worth.
- Wages, employment, productivity, production, business formation, and capital expenditure.
- Supply chains, infrastructure, energy, transport, trade, and physical capacity as links between finance and production.
- Financial conditions and their possible effects on investment, employment, prices, output, and resilience.
- Distribution of gains, losses, access, costs, and risks across groups and sectors.

### 3.9 Financial infrastructure and intermediaries
- Central banks, commercial banks, non-bank financial institutions, exchanges, payment providers, clearing houses, custodians, brokers, rating agencies, and financial-data providers.
- Payment rails, settlement systems, collateral arrangements, correspondent networks, and market access.
- Intermediation chains and the roles of originator, lender, investor, custodian, manager, guarantor, borrower, and end beneficiary.
- Operational concentration, legal jurisdictions, regulatory boundaries, and infrastructure dependencies.

### 3.10 Information, institutions, incentives, and governance
- Statistical definitions, reporting standards, disclosure rules, regulatory requirements, and data gaps.
- Ownership, governance, mandates, incentives, conflicts, constraints, and decision authority.
- Information delays, revisions, non-public information boundaries, and unequal access to information.
- How policies, institutional decisions, expectations, and narratives may affect financial behaviour.
- Treat incentives as clues to investigate, not proof of intention or misconduct.

### 3.11 Risk, resilience, and system-wide interactions
- Liquidity, solvency, credit, market, currency, interest-rate, maturity, concentration, operational, and sovereign risks.
- Network exposures, correlated holdings, common funding sources, feedback loops, and potential contagion channels.
- Asset-price declines, collateral calls, funding stress, refinancing shocks, and forced selling as possible mechanisms to investigate.
- Buffers, reserves, insurance, hedging, diversification, backstops, and resolution mechanisms.
- Stress scenarios and historical episodes, without assuming a past relationship must recur.

### 3.12 Distribution, access, and allocation
- Where resources are concentrated and where financing constraints appear.
- Who can access funds, on what terms, for what purposes, and over what time horizon.
- Differences between wealth concentration, financial inclusion, credit access, investment allocation, and economic opportunity.
- Geographic, sectoral, demographic, institutional, and currency dimensions where data and ethical use permit.
- Whether capital is flowing to new production, asset purchases, refinancing, consumption, public obligations, or other uses.
- “Misallocation” must be defined against an explicit benchmark; it is not established merely by observing unequal distributions or a large balance.

### 3.13 Possible applications to keep open
- Global financial-state and balance-sheet mapping.
- Country, sector, institution, and instrument comparisons.
- Capital-flow and funding-condition monitoring.
- Fiscal and policy analysis, including subsidies, public investment, debt, and distributional consequences.
- Financial stability and interconnected exposure analysis.
- FX, rates, bond, commodity, equity, and other market research.
- Investment and sector research.
- Business and intelligence services built around decision-relevant, verified information.
- Measurement research, source comparison, and detection of missing or inconsistent data.
- Other applications discovered through evidence. This list is not a commitment to build every capability.

## 4. Analytical views to keep distinct

For any subject, consider which views apply rather than forcing every question into one metric.

| View | Question |
|---|---|
| Stock | What balance or position exists at a defined date? |
| Flow | What transactions or changes occurred over a defined period? |
| Ownership | Who legally or beneficially owns the position, as supported by evidence? |
| Control | Who can decide how the asset or funds are used? |
| Liability | What obligation corresponds to an asset or financing arrangement? |
| Availability | What can actually be mobilized for a specified purpose and time horizon? |
| Liquidity | How readily can an asset be converted or a payment obligation met, under stated conditions? |
| Allocation | Which sectors, instruments, activities, or recipients receive financing? |
| Valuation | How much change comes from transactions versus changing prices or exchange rates? |
| Transmission | Through what mechanism might a change affect other entities or outcomes? |
| Distribution | Who receives benefits, bears costs, or faces constraints? |
| Risk | What exposure, vulnerability, buffer, or dependency is evidenced? |
| Observability | What is directly measured, estimated, inferred, delayed, undisclosed, or missing? |

## 5. Measurement and accounting safeguards

1. **Stocks and flows are different.** A balance at a date is not the same as transactions during a period.
2. **Money, credit, wealth, and capital are not synonyms.** Define the object before measuring it.
3. **Gross and net positions differ.** Assets and liabilities should be visible separately before any netting.
4. **Avoid double-counting.** A deposit is an asset to its holder and a liability to the bank; a bond is an asset to the holder and a liability to the issuer. Consolidated totals must follow an explicit sector boundary and accounting method.
5. **Financial claims are not automatically additional real wealth.** A financial asset and its matching liability may cancel when sectors are consolidated, while the underlying real asset and the distribution of claims still matter.
6. **Valuation changes are not transactions.** Separate purchases, sales, repayments, new issuance, write-offs, exchange-rate effects, and price revaluations where possible.
7. **Coverage and classification matter.** Specify resident versus nationality basis, institutional sector, instrument, currency, valuation method, maturity, date, and reporting boundary.
8. **Liquidity is conditional.** An asset's market value does not establish that it can be converted quickly at that value or used for a particular payment.
9. **Commitments are not deployed funds.** Distinguish announcements, pledges, commitments, approvals, disbursements, investments, and realized proceeds.
10. **Fiscal savings are counterfactual.** A policy change does not automatically free an equal amount of cash for another purpose; trace the budget mechanics and other effects.
11. **Global sums require reconciliation.** Do not add incompatible statistics or force a single “world money” total from overlapping aggregates.
12. **Uncertainty remains explicit.** Publish coverage gaps, estimates, revisions, source conflicts, and confidence; absence of a public figure is not evidence that the underlying quantity is zero.

## 6. Candidate source families to investigate

This is a discovery list, not a completed source audit or guarantee that each source contains every required variable.

- **IMF:** monetary and financial statistics, international financial statistics, balance of payments, international investment positions, fiscal data, and coordinated portfolio/investment surveys where relevant.
- **Bank for International Settlements (BIS):** international banking, debt securities, credit, derivatives, and selected global liquidity or financial-market statistics.
- **World Bank:** national accounts, development finance, debt, poverty, and country-level economic datasets.
- **OECD:** institutional-investor, pension, insurance, household, and financial statistics for covered economies and topics.
- **National statistical agencies:** GDP, household, corporate, sectoral, and national balance-sheet statistics where published.
- **Central banks and monetary authorities:** balance sheets, monetary aggregates, bank credit, reserves, rates, and financial stability reports.
- **Finance ministries, debt-management offices, and treasuries:** budgets, debt stock and issuance, cash management, fiscal execution, and public-sector reports.
- **Financial regulators and deposit-insurance authorities:** banking, insurance, pension, securities, and non-bank financial-sector data.
- **Fund managers, pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, and institutional disclosures:** holdings, assets under management, mandates, commitments, and performance, subject to reporting limits.
- **Exchanges and market-data providers:** security prices, issuance, trading, yields, bid/ask quotes, volumes, and market depth, subject to access, cost, licensing, and coverage.
- **Company filings and registries:** corporate financial statements, ownership disclosures, debt, investment, and transactions where disclosed.
- **Official international and national reports:** public finance, development finance, subsidies, public investment, and cross-border capital.
- **Other verifiable public or licensed sources:** consider only after documenting provenance, access rights, definition, timeliness, and limitations.

For every source and series, record the source owner, direct URL or document reference, definition, units, currency, institutional/geographic coverage, date and frequency, publication lag, revision policy, methodology, access/licensing conditions, missingness, and known limitations. Compare definitions before combining sources.

## 7. Core research questions

Maintain these as an open question bank; add, split, reframe, answer, or retire questions as evidence develops.

### Global totals and boundaries
- What quantities can be credibly estimated globally: household wealth, real assets, financial assets, money aggregates, credit, debt, institutional assets, and cross-border positions?
- Which global totals are comparable and which are incompatible or overlapping?
- What is known about private, public, and institutional balance sheets, and where are the largest coverage gaps?
- How do national balance sheets, financial accounts, and international investment positions fit together?

### Distribution and control
- How are assets and liabilities distributed among households, businesses, governments, financial institutions, and non-financial institutions?
- What is known about ownership and control, and what remains unobservable from public data?
- Where are financial resources concentrated by country, currency, institution, sector, instrument, and maturity?
- Which actors are holders, managers, custodians, intermediaries, borrowers, issuers, or final beneficiaries?

### Money, credit, and liquidity
- Which monetary aggregates are being measured, by whom, and under what definitions?
- How do bank deposits, reserves, loans, securities, and non-bank credit relate?
- Where do funding constraints exist despite apparently large financial balances?
- How do credit creation, repayments, refinancing, and changes in risk appetite alter financial positions?

### Capital pools and allocation
- How much capital is reported as assets, cash, commitments, uncalled commitments, deployed capital, or realizable proceeds?
- How do fund mandates, regulation, liquidity needs, risk limits, and investment horizons constrain allocation?
- How do announced and completed investments differ?
- Which flows finance new productive activity, existing asset transfers, refinancing, or other uses?

### Countries and cross-border flows
- How do domestic balance sheets connect to international claims and liabilities?
- How do currencies, interest-rate differences, exchange-rate changes, controls, and settlement access affect capital movement?
- What can be inferred from official reserves and international investment positions, and what cannot?
- How do capital flows relate to trade, remittances, direct investment, portfolio investment, and cross-border bank funding?

### Governments and public policy
- How do revenue, expenditure, borrowing, debt service, cash management, subsidies, and public investment fit together?
- What are the direct budget flows and wider transmission effects of a particular policy?
- How much of a claimed fiscal saving is actually realized, when, and under what assumptions?
- What are the distributional consequences and feasible alternatives, including their costs, financing, timing, and constraints?

### Markets, risk, and transmission
- How do central-bank actions, fiscal choices, market repricing, collateral, and funding conditions transmit across institutions and countries?
- Which links are directly measured, and which are plausible mechanisms requiring tests?
- Where do common holdings, concentrated exposures, maturity mismatch, currency mismatch, or funding dependencies create vulnerability?
- How do observed flows differ from price changes and changes in valuation?

### Evidence and methodology
- Which data are authoritative, timely, comparable, reproducible, and affordable to access?
- Which source disagreements result from different definitions, timing, coverage, revisions, or actual inconsistencies?
- What can be reconciled across sectors and countries, and at what cost?
- Which quantities cannot currently be measured reliably, and what proxies—if any—are defensible?

### Applications and priorities
- Which views answer meaningful questions for research, public policy, investment, trading, business, or intelligence services?
- What evidence would demonstrate that an analysis improves a decision?
- Which investigations can produce useful learning with accessible public data and modest resources?
- Which directions should remain exploratory, be deferred, or be abandoned because the evidence or practical value is insufficient?

## 8. Initial research process

1. **Inventory concepts and questions.** Expand this map without treating the initial taxonomy as complete.
2. **Define measurement objects.** For each proposed quantity, specify the unit, sector, boundary, stock/flow status, valuation basis, currency, and date.
3. **Audit data sources.** Identify actual series and documents, check definitions and access, and record coverage and limitations.
4. **Choose small overlapping samples.** Test a manageable number of countries, sectors, instruments, and dates before attempting global aggregation.
5. **Reconcile where possible.** Compare counterpart assets and liabilities, sector totals, and independent sources. Document residuals and reasons they may exist.
6. **Create separate views.** Keep stocks, flows, wealth, monetary aggregates, credit, capital-pool assets, fiscal flows, market valuations, and real-economy measures distinct until their relationships are clear.
7. **Investigate connections.** Map relevant entities, roles, mechanisms, transmission paths, feedback, and alternative explanations.
8. **Record evidence status.** Label data as verified, provisional, estimated, illustrative, inferred, contradictory, or missing.
9. **Review what is learned.** Update the concept, measurement, source, relationship, and question registers.
10. **Prioritize only from evidence.** Use data quality, explanatory value, feasibility, learning value, and practical relevance to decide where deeper work is warranted.

Do not begin by building a global dashboard or a single grand equation. First establish defensible definitions and data boundaries. Visualizations, calculations, automation, and specialized applications can follow when they serve a clearly specified question.

## 9. Initial milestones

- **Milestone 1 — Scope and definitions:** Establish a shared vocabulary and distinguish stock, flow, wealth, money, credit, liquidity, capital, asset, liability, and financial claim.
- **Milestone 2 — Source inventory:** Build a source-and-series register for the major data families; verify actual availability and comparability.
- **Milestone 3 — Pilot balance-sheet map:** Select a small number of sectors or countries and test how the data fit together without claiming full global coverage.
- **Milestone 4 — Pilot flow map:** Trace selected financing, investment, fiscal, or cross-border flows and distinguish transactions from valuation effects.
- **Milestone 5 — Connection and transmission tests:** Investigate a few relationships with explicit evidence and alternatives.
- **Milestone 6 — Review and reprioritize:** Document what is measurable, what remains unknown, what deserves deeper research, and what practical applications may be justified.

These milestones are provisional. They are intended to organize discovery, not impose a rigid sequence or prevent useful parallel investigations.

## 10. Current status and open limitations

- This is a research map, not a measured picture of current global finance.
- No current global total is asserted here.
- Candidate source families have not all been audited or ingested.
- A comprehensive, reconciled global balance sheet is not assumed to be achievable from public data alone.
- Institutional reports may use different definitions, coverage, valuations, and reporting dates.
- Ownership, control, liquidity, and deployability are often not fully observable from headline figures.
- Causal pathways must be investigated link by link; co-movement, plausible incentives, or network proximity do not prove causation or intent.
- The taxonomy, question bank, sources, and priorities should evolve as real evidence accumulates.

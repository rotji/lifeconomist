# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 16–18

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 16–18 against Concepts 22, 24 and 25 and related notes.  
**Purpose:** Confirm conceptual coverage, repair concrete explanatory gaps in existing notes, and separate conceptual mechanisms from current data or institution-specific conclusions.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and its principal boundaries.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears but needs an important mechanism or boundary clarified.
- **Cross-linked:** Another note is the primary home; preserve a link rather than duplicate it.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed notes.

## 2. Group 16 — Trade, supply chains and distribution networks

**Primary note:** [Concept 22 — Trade, Supply Chains and Distribution Networks](concepts/trade-supply-chains-and-distribution-networks.md). Related notes include Concepts 12, 17–19, 24–25 and 29.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic and international trade | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 distinguishes transaction flows, domestic/international movement and relevant legal and border conditions. A shipment record alone does not prove origin, ownership transfer or final payment. |
| Comparative advantage, specialization and trade costs | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section explains opportunity cost and potential gains, while distinguishing theoretical advantage from commercially viable trade and from how gains are distributed. |
| Supply chains, value chains and production networks | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 separates the physical supply network, value-creating stages and logistics/distribution links. They overlap but are not synonyms. |
| Procurement, logistics, transport, storage and inventory | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 covers sourcing, transport, inventory, throughput, capacity, storage and bottlenecks. Actual exposure requires product- and route-specific data. |
| Wholesale, retail, margins and distribution services | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 explains intermediary functions, channels, markups and margins. Intermediary margins alone do not prove inefficiency or exploitation. |
| Import dependence, local content and substitution | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section separates imported final goods from intermediate inputs, domestic value added, local assembly, technology and ownership. Domestic substitution is not automatically cheaper or more resilient. |
| Bottlenecks, concentration and critical dependencies | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 covers constraints, concentration and disruption propagation. Criticality depends on substitutability, lead time, stock, capacity and consequences of failure. |
| Trade finance, letters of credit and settlement | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section explains documentary credit, the conditional nature of bank undertakings and the distinction between issuance, shipment, document compliance, payment and final settlement. |
| Input-output tables and limits of aggregate tracing | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section explains industry-level input-output relationships, aggregation and assumptions, and warns that modelled indirect effects are not firm-level observed transactions. |
| Supply shocks, propagation and resilience | Explicitly covered | Concept 22 covers disruption and recovery. A plausible propagation path is not proof of observed downstream impact; evidence of exposure and timing is needed. |

### Group 16 conclusion

Group 16 is conceptually covered. Targeted additions clarify comparative advantage, trade finance, local-content claims and input-output model limitations. Remaining work is empirical: define product and geographic boundaries, use current trade and customs data, distinguish domestic value added from gross trade values, and verify actual contracts, shipments, payments and dependencies. Avoid double-counting the same good at multiple supply-chain stages.

## 3. Group 17 — Banking liquidity, market liquidity and system-wide liquidity

**Primary note:** [Concept 24 — Banking Liquidity, Market Liquidity and System-Wide Liquidity](concepts/banking-liquidity-market-liquidity-and-system-wide-liquidity.md). Related notes include Concepts 10, 17–19, 21 and 25.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Cash availability versus accounting wealth | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 distinguishes immediately usable cash from wealth, accounting assets and resources that require sale or approval. |
| Bank reserves, deposits and central-bank money | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 explains reserve balances and deposits as different claims. Definitions and eligible participants depend on the monetary and settlement framework. |
| Funding liquidity versus market liquidity | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 explains the ability to obtain funding versus the ability to trade assets without excessive price impact. They can reinforce each other but are not identical. |
| Collateral availability and collateral quality | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 distinguishes market value from eligibility, encumbrance, legal enforceability, haircut and actual usability. |
| Repo funding, haircuts, margin calls and fire sales | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concepts 18 and 24 cover secured funding, margin and pressured sales. Specific causal claims require transaction terms, timing and price/volume evidence. |
| Money-market funds and short-term funding | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section explains the MMF investor-to-borrower channel, fund shares versus deposits, redemption pressure and possible asset-sale/funding transmission. Legal protections and redemption tools vary by jurisdiction. |
| Payment settlement and intraday liquidity | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section explains timing gaps, queued payments, intraday credit/collateral and the difference between an instruction and final settlement. End-of-day balances may miss intraday stress. |
| Liquidity creation, withdrawal and transmission | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 explains liquidity movement through banking, market and counterparty networks. Mechanisms should not be presented as proof of a particular causal event without evidence. |
| Liquidity stress versus insolvency | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 separates inability to meet payments at the required time from a deeper inability to meet obligations or absorb losses. They can interact but are not interchangeable. |
| System-wide liquidity indicators and interpretation limits | Explicitly covered | Concept 24 provides indicators and limits, emphasizing dates, entity boundaries, currency, horizon and definitions. No single ratio establishes system-wide liquidity conditions. |

### Group 17 conclusion

Group 17 is conceptually covered. Targeted additions address intraday timing and money-market funds. Remaining work is to define the relevant settlement system, facility rules, payment statuses, fund mandate, portfolio, redemption terms and data horizon before diagnosing an actual liquidity event. A plausible transmission mechanism is not itself evidence of systemic stress.

## 4. Group 18 — Capital markets, institutional investors and investment funds

**Primary note:** [Concept 25 — Capital Markets, Institutional Investors and Investment Funds](concepts/capital-markets-institutional-investors-and-investment-funds.md). Related notes include Concepts 11, 18–21, 24 and 31.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Pension funds, insurers, mutual funds and exchange-traded funds | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 explains institutional investor types and different liabilities, mandates and fund structures. Legal and investment rules vary by jurisdiction. |
| Private equity, venture capital and private credit | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 covers these fund types and their different investment strategies, control arrangements and liquidity profiles. Strategy labels do not establish a specific portfolio or behavior. |
| Sovereign wealth funds, endowments and foundations | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 distinguishes public and mission-based pools of capital from other fund structures. Source of capital, mandate and governance must be verified case by case. |
| Assets under management versus assets owned or controlled | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 distinguishes managed assets, legal ownership, custody and exposure. AUM is not the manager's own balance-sheet wealth. |
| Limited partners, general partners and fund structures | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 separates investor commitments, manager authority, governance and fund-level claims. The precise legal arrangement depends on documents and jurisdiction. |
| Fund commitments, capital calls, deployment and distributions | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 distinguishes commitments from paid-in capital, investment deployment, realized proceeds and distributions. An announced commitment is not cash already invested. |
| Mandates, benchmarks, allocation and portfolio rebalancing | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 distinguishes strategic allocation, tactical decisions, benchmark comparison and rebalancing. A holding alone does not prove motive or discretionary authority. |
| Custody, nominee holdings and beneficial ownership | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 separates custody and nominee registration from underlying economic ownership and control. Registry and filing rules determine available evidence. |
| Valuation frequency, illiquidity and stale prices | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New section distinguishes modelled or stale marks from realizable sale value, and requires valuation date, method, market depth, restrictions and realization evidence. |
| Investor influence versus legal ownership and operational control | Explicitly covered | Concept 25 distinguishes influence, governance rights, economic exposure and direct operation. Actual authority depends on contracts, voting rights, mandates and practice. |

### Group 18 conclusion

Group 18 is conceptually covered. The targeted addition makes valuation frequency and realizable value more explicit. Remaining work is fund-specific evidence: governing documents, audited reports, capital-call and distribution history, portfolio holdings, valuation policies, fees, liquidity restrictions, custody arrangements and applicable legal rules. Reported AUM, commitments, valuation marks and realized cash should remain distinct.

## 6. Remaining evidence and measurement work

- **Trade:** Verify commodity/product codes, quantities, values, origin, import content, customs treatment, shipment and settlement milestones. State table year and assumptions for input-output analysis.
- **Liquidity:** Verify payment timestamps and finality, eligible settlement assets, collateral encumbrance, facility terms, market depth, funding costs and relevant fund redemption rules.
- **Investment funds:** Verify legal entity and fund structure, audited financial statements, commitments versus paid-in capital, deployment/distributions, valuation date and method, custody and governing rights.
- **Evidence discipline:** Distinguish definitions and plausible mechanisms from observed transactions, measured outcomes, causal findings and jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions. Record source date, scope, uncertainty and competing explanations.

# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 10–12

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 10–12 in the Workstream A roadmap, against Concepts 17–19 and relevant connected notes.  
**Purpose:** Confirm conceptual coverage, identify explanatory gaps and distinguish note improvements from formal measurement and current Nigerian source verification. This audit does not validate current Nigerian FX, market or company-accounting statistics.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and its principal boundaries.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears, but an important mechanism or distinction needs clearer treatment.
- **Cross-linked:** Another concept is the primary home; preserve a link instead of duplicating content.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed notes.

Conceptual coverage is not a substitute for a formal indicator definition, an applicable legal/accounting rule or current-source verification.

## 2. Group 10 — Foreign exchange, international money and global capital flows

**Primary note:** [Concept 17 — Foreign Exchange, International Money and Global Capital Flows](concepts/foreign-exchange-international-money-and-global-capital-flows.md). Related notes include Concepts 10–12, 16, 18, 24 and 29.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Currency, exchange rates and quote conventions | Explicitly covered | Concept 17 distinguishes currency, quote orientation, currency value and exchange-rate conventions. Correct pair orientation remains essential to any calculation. |
| Spot, forward, swap and other FX transactions | Explicitly covered conceptually | Concept 17 covers spot/forward and other transactions. Actual settlement terms, market conventions and access depend on the instrument and venue. |
| Nominal, real, bilateral and effective exchange rates | Explicitly covered | Concept 17 distinguishes bilateral rates and effective indices and explains why inflation-adjusted and nominal measures answer different questions. |
| Appreciation, depreciation, devaluation and revaluation | Explicitly covered | Concept 17 distinguishes market-driven movements from official parity changes under relevant regimes. |
| Cross rates, bid/ask spreads and conversion friction | **Explicitly covered after targeted addition** | Concept 17 now distinguishes theoretical cross rates from executable conversions and explains quote orientation, two-leg conversion costs, access and settlement conditions. |
| Current account, capital account and financial account | Explicitly covered | Concept 17 explains the balance-of-payments structure and warns against treating the capital account as synonymous with all capital flows. |
| Cross-border assets, liabilities, claims and flows | Explicitly covered | Concept 17 distinguishes transaction flows from stocks and valuation changes; the international investment position is a stock framework. |
| Foreign direct investment, portfolio investment and remittances | **FDI and portfolio investment explicitly covered; remittance distinction strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 17 explains investment functional categories and now clarifies that remittance transfers, payment instructions and completed transfers are not interchangeable, and exact statistical coverage depends on the framework. |
| Capital flight, inflows/outflows and reserve assets | **In/outflows and reserves explicitly covered; capital-flight interpretation strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 17 now cautions that capital flight is not a single universally defined series and that gross outflows or falling reserves alone do not prove it. |
| Carry trades, currency risk and settlement constraints | **Carry and currency risk explicitly covered; settlement constraints strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 17 covers carry and currency mismatch and now adds payment timing, currency availability, correspondent-bank, compliance and settlement-finality considerations. |

### Group 10 conclusion

Group 10 is conceptually covered. The additions make a key distinction explicit: a calculated exchange-rate relationship is not necessarily an executable transaction. Remaining work is empirical and operational—verify current official series, define quote orientation, and document rate type, access route, bid/ask, transaction size, costs, settlement and data vintage before drawing conclusions about Nigerian FX markets or external flows.

## 3. Group 11 — Financial markets and market infrastructure

### Group 11 conclusion

Group 11 is conceptually covered. The targeted addition strengthens fixed-income risk measures and secured funding. Remaining work concerns formal market data and instrument-specific analysis: define the curve or security, yield convention, maturity, collateral, haircut, counterparty, margin terms and settlement rules. A high repo volume or a yield-curve shape alone does not establish systemic risk or a unique macroeconomic cause.

### Group 12 conclusion

Group 12 is conceptually covered. The additions make it easier to diagnose situations where reported profit coexists with cash stress, or where a guarantee or other contingent exposure creates future risk without matching the headline debt figure. Remaining work requires the applicable accounting and tax framework, entity boundary, reporting period, audit status, notes/disclosures and consistent definitions. No accounting term should be treated as universal across every jurisdiction or reporting framework.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 17 — Foreign Exchange, International Money and Global Capital Flows:** Added (a) cross rates, executable spreads and conversion friction; and (b) remittances, capital flight and settlement constraints. These sections distinguish theoretical rates, transaction stages, official statistical categories and inferred motives.
2. **Concept 18 — Financial Markets and Market Infrastructure:** Added (a) bond yields, duration, convexity and term structure; and (b) repo, collateral reuse and securities lending. These sections explain assumptions, collateral/haircut mechanics, reuse chains and limits on interpreting market indicators.
3. **Concept 19 — Accounting, Balance Sheets and Financial Statements:** Added (a) working capital, operating cycle and cash conversion; (b) accounting profit, taxable profit and cash generated; and (c) off-balance-sheet exposures and contingent obligations. These sections clarify timing, tax/reporting boundaries and contingent triggers.

No current Nigerian FX rates, market prices, yields, company figures or accounting-policy determinations were added or asserted as verified.

## 6. Remaining measurement and evidence work

- **FX and external sector:** Verify current CBN/NBS and other relevant official definitions, rate series, units, pair orientation, reporting basis, valuation changes, release vintage and revisions. Use time-aligned executable quotes and all-in costs for transaction-level claims.
- **Market infrastructure:** Identify the exact instrument and market; verify issuer, currency, maturity, yield convention, collateral terms, market access and settlement arrangements. Separate observed data from calculated measures and causal explanations.
- **Company accounts:** Verify reporting entity, framework, period, audit status, recognition policies, tax rules, cash restrictions, related parties, commitments and note disclosures. Reconcile stocks and flows rather than substituting one for another.
- **Evidence discipline:** Separate a reported number, an accounting/statistical definition, a plausible mechanism and a verified causal or legal conclusion. Record limitations and competing explanations.

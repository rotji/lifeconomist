# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 19–21

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 19–21 against Concepts 26–28 and related notes.  
**Purpose:** Confirm conceptual coverage, strengthen consequential explanatory gaps in existing notes, and keep mechanisms distinct from empirical claims about specific countries, industries, energy systems or AI deployments.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and its principal boundaries.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears but an important mechanism or boundary needs clarification.
- **Cross-linked:** Another concept is the primary home; link rather than duplicate.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed notes.

## 2. Group 19 — Economic growth, development and productivity

**Primary note:** [Concept 26 — Economic Growth, Development and Structural Transformation](concepts/economic-growth-development-and-structural-transformation.md). Related concepts include Concepts 6–8, 13, 15, 23 and 27.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth versus economic development | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 distinguishes measured output expansion from broader capabilities and living conditions. |
| Output per person and productivity growth | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 separates total output, population-adjusted output and productivity; Concept 7 is the detailed productivity reference. |
| Capital deepening, skills and technological progress | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 covers complementary inputs and capability; the existence of investment or technology alone does not establish realized productivity gains. |
| Total-factor productivity and measurement caveats | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 explains growth-accounting residuals and why they are not automatically a causal measure of technology or efficiency. |
| Structural change, urbanization and formalization | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 discusses sectoral reallocation and the limits of using sector shares as a complete development measure. |
| Infrastructure, energy access and public capability | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concept 26 covers infrastructure and public capability; Concept 27 is the primary home for energy conversion, delivery and reliability. |
| Innovation, diffusion and organizational capability | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 distinguishes invention, adoption, diffusion and organizational routines. |
| Human development, welfare and distribution | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 explains why aggregate growth does not establish gains in household welfare, public services or distribution. |
| Convergence, divergence and middle-income transitions | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 covers catch-up and convergence distinctions; country-specific classification and causal explanation need separate evidence. |
| Growth drivers versus growth-accounting contributions | Explicitly covered | Concept 26 explicitly distinguishes accounting decomposition from causal inference. |
| Employment absorption, job quality and productivity | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New subsection explains that output growth and employment growth may diverge; it distinguishes workers from hours and aggregate job counts from job quality, underemployment and distribution. |

### Group 19 conclusion

Group 19 is conceptually covered, with a targeted addition on employment absorption and job quality. The remaining work is empirical: align real output, hours, employment, wages and labor-force definitions; examine sector and group differences; and avoid interpreting growth-accounting contributions as causal effects. A rise in output per worker does not alone prove improved job quality or broadly shared welfare.

### Group 20 conclusion

Group 20 is conceptually covered, with a targeted addition on energy affordability and full system costs. Remaining work is to measure the actual service received, total costs, reliability, customer classes and distributional incidence. A low posted tariff does not necessarily mean low total cost or adequate service; a higher tariff does not alone establish efficiency, fairness or reliability.

## 4. Group 21 — Digital, information and intangible economics

**Primary note:** [Concept 28 — Digital, Information and Intangible Economics](concepts/digital-information-and-intangible-economics.md). Related concepts include Concepts 6–8, 18, 20, 22 and 31.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|

| Low marginal cost versus total operating cost | Explicitly covered | Concept 28 covers development, infrastructure, security, maintenance, support and failure costs. |
| AI service unit economics and useful outcomes | **Strengthened by targeted addition** | New subsection requires a defined unit such as an accepted task or useful outcome and compares the complete workflow, including review and error handling, with the baseline. |

### Group 21 conclusion

Group 21 is conceptually covered, with a targeted addition on AI service unit economics. Remaining work is application-specific measurement: define the task and outcome, workload, model/version, quality threshold, utilization, human-review burden, failure severity, baseline process and full cost boundary. Token or request cost is not a sufficient proxy for cost per accepted outcome or economic value.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 26 — Economic Growth, Development and Structural Transformation:** Added “Employment absorption, job quality and productivity,” covering output versus job growth, workers versus hours, job quality, skills, wage outcomes and distribution.
2. **Concept 27 — Natural Resources, Energy and Environmental Economics:** Added “Energy affordability, tariffs and full system costs,” covering posted prices versus effective/full costs, reliability, pass-through and distributional incidence.
3. **Concept 28 — Digital, Information and Intangible Economics:** Added “AI service economics: training, inference, utilization and human review,” covering total cost per defined useful outcome, utilization and comparison with a baseline workflow.

No country-specific growth statistics, energy tariffs, subsidy-incidence results, AI benchmark claims or commercial viability claims were added or asserted as verified.

## 6. Remaining evidence and measurement work

- **Growth and employment:** Align national-accounting period, real-output measure, employment definition, hours, participation, wages and sector coverage; separate observed outcomes from causal explanations.
- **Energy and environment:** Define energy carrier and customer class; distinguish posted, paid and full-service costs; measure outages and actual service; verify tariff/subsidy rules and their implementation.
- **Digital and AI:** Record task, baseline, unit, model/version, utilization, quality, error severity, human intervention, compute and operational costs, and the period used to allocate fixed costs.
- **Evidence discipline:** Record source dates, units, boundaries, uncertainty and competing explanations. A mechanism or illustrative example is not evidence that a real-world outcome occurred.

## 7. Audit decision and next step

Groups 19–21 are **conceptually covered**, with targeted additions made to Concepts 26, 27 and 28. Remaining work is primarily formal measurement and evidence-based application to specific economies, energy systems and digital services. No new numbered concept is justified by this audit.

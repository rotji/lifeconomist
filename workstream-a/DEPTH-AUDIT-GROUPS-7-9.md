# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 7–9

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 7–9 in the Workstream A roadmap, against Concepts 14–16 and relevant connected notes.  
**Purpose:** Identify conceptual omissions, confirm existing coverage, and distinguish concept-note improvements from Workstream B measurement or country-specific verification. This audit does not validate current Nigerian labour, fiscal or inflation statistics.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and its main conceptual boundary.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears, but an important distinction or mechanism needs clearer treatment.
- **Cross-linked:** Another note is the primary home for the topic; link rather than duplicate it.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed notes.

A conceptual explanation does not establish that a statistic is available, current or methodologically comparable. Country-specific claims require official source and metadata verification.

## 2. Group 7 — Labour, employment and household economics

**Primary note:** [Concept 14 — Labour, Employment and Household Economics](concepts/labour-employment-and-household-economics.md). Related notes include Concepts 6, 7, 9, 13 and 16.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Labour force, employment, unemployment and underemployment | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 explains status definitions, reference periods, labour-force participation, unemployment, time-related underemployment and broader labour underutilization. |
| Wages, salaries, compensation and labour income | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 distinguishes wages, earnings, compensation of employees, gross and net pay, and the difficulty of separating labour and capital income in self-employment. |
| Hours worked, skills, human capital and experience | Explicitly covered conceptually; formal skill measures remain context-specific | Concept 14 separates headcount from hours worked and discusses skills and human-capital connections. Operational skill, experience and quality measures depend on the data and framework. |
| Labour productivity and unit labour costs | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concepts 7 and 14 explain output per worker/hour and unit labour cost, while warning that the measures depend on output, hours, compensation and composition. Formal calculations require aligned series. |
| Household income, expenditure, saving and debt | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 distinguishes income sources, disposable income, consumption expenditure, saving as a flow, accumulated savings as a stock, borrowing and asset sales. |
| Consumption, needs, preferences and budget constraints | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 covers household consumption, living standards, resource constraints and differences in needs. A full behavioural model would depend on the specific research question. |
| Poverty, living standards, welfare and distribution | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 distinguishes monetary, relative, deprivation and multidimensional poverty concepts, living standards and inequality. Comparisons require consistent thresholds and welfare aggregates. |
| Care work, unpaid household activity and time use | Explicitly covered | Concept 14 explains unpaid care and household services, their importance and their treatment in headline employment/GDP measures; time-use and satellite accounts may add visibility. |
| Demographics, dependency and participation rates | **Explicitly covered after targeted addition** | Concept 14 now distinguishes age dependency from economic dependency and explains why a demographic dividend is a potential conditioned by jobs, skills, health, investment and institutions—not an automatic gain. |

### Group 7 conclusion

Group 7 is conceptually covered. The targeted addition clarifies demographic dependency and the demographic dividend. Remaining work is mainly formal measurement: confirm labour-force definitions, age bands, hours, household welfare aggregates, poverty thresholds, time-use coverage and the official Nigerian statistical framework before building indicators. A lower unemployment rate should not be interpreted without participation and employment-to-population measures.

### Group 8 conclusion

Group 8 is conceptually covered, with the addition on fiscal space, debt sustainability and sovereign wealth funds. Remaining tasks include verifying official Nigerian budget and debt definitions, fiscal reporting boundaries, the treatment of public enterprises and funds, and the actual authority and execution chain for a given expenditure. A debt ratio alone cannot establish either immediate liquidity or overall sustainability.

## 4. Group 9 — Inflation, interest rates and purchasing power

**Primary note:** [Concept 16 — Inflation, Interest Rates and Purchasing Power](concepts/inflation-interest-rates-and-purchasing-power.md). Related notes include Concepts 4, 9–13, 15, 17, 18, 24 and 30.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation, disinflation and deflation | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 defines each and distinguishes the rate of change from the price level. |
| Price levels, relative prices and cost of living | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 separates individual prices, aggregate indexes and household-specific cost pressure, including basket and weight limitations. |
| Nominal and real interest rates | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 explains approximate and exact relationships, expected versus realized inflation and the need to align horizons. |
| Policy rates, lending rates, deposit rates and bond yields | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 explains that policy rates influence but do not mechanically set market rates, which also reflect risk, maturity, liquidity, funding and competition. |
| Inflation expectations and real purchasing power | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 distinguishes expected from realized inflation and connects purchasing power to the relevant basket, period and income. |
| Wage-price dynamics and pass-through | **Mechanisms strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 16 already covers cost and exchange-rate pass-through; the new subsection makes wage-price feedback, its conditionality and possible second-round effects more explicit. |
| Indexation, administered prices and measurement baskets | **Explicitly covered after targeted addition** | Concept 16 now explains indexation, administered-price changes, direct index effects and the distinction between a one-time price-level effect and persistent inflation. |
| Distributional effects of inflation | Explicitly covered | Concept 16 explains why effects vary with household baskets, wages, transfers, location and debt; actual distributional claims require disaggregated evidence. |
| Monetary tightening/easing and transmission mechanisms | Explicitly covered conceptually; causal effect estimates remain context-specific | Concept 16 covers policy-to-market-rate and financial-market channels and lags. Quantifying transmission requires time-aligned data and a defensible causal design. |

### Group 9 conclusion

Group 9 is conceptually covered, with the new section on wage-price dynamics, indexation and administered prices. Remaining work is empirical: verify the current Nigerian CPI and interest-rate methodologies, define the relevant policy and market rates, and test specific inflation explanations against alternative drivers and time-aligned data. A price increase, exchange-rate movement or wage change alone does not prove the cause or persistence of broad inflation.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 14 — Labour, Employment and Household Economics:** Added “Demographics, dependency and the demographic dividend.” It separates age ratios from economic dependency, explains conditional demographic-dividend mechanisms, notes ageing-related channels, and specifies what to document when using dependency statistics.
2. **Concept 15 — Government Finance, Taxation and Public Institutions:** Added “Fiscal space, sustainability and sovereign wealth funds.” It distinguishes fiscal space from a single ratio, frames debt sustainability around assumptions and stress scenarios, explains different SWF mandates, and separates headline fund assets from immediately available budget resources.
3. **Concept 16 — Inflation, Interest Rates and Purchasing Power:** Added “Wage-price dynamics, indexation and administered prices.” It explains conditional wage/price feedback, indexation design, direct administered-price effects and the possible but uncertain propagation into persistent inflation.

No current Nigerian labour, fiscal, CPI or interest-rate statistics were added or claimed as verified.

## 6. Remaining measurement and evidence work

- **Labour:** Verify the current NBS labour-force framework, age range, reference periods, labour-underutilization measures, survey coverage and revision history. Use household and time-use evidence for distribution and unpaid work questions.
- **Fiscal:** State the government boundary, cash/accrual basis, gross/consolidated presentation, domestic/external debt definition, reporting vintage and treatment of guarantees and public funds. Trace authorizations through commitments, payments, physical delivery and outcomes.
- **Inflation:** Verify official CPI coverage, weights, base/reference period, formula and revisions. Select an appropriate price index for real income or interest-rate questions.
- **Causal claims:** Distinguish a measured movement, a plausible mechanism, a causal estimate and a policy judgement. Document timing, competing explanations and uncertainty.

## 7. Audit decision and next step

Groups 7–9 are **conceptually covered**, with targeted additions made to Concepts 14–16. No new numbered concept is justified by this audit. The remaining work is mainly formal measurement, current-source verification and application to specific questions.

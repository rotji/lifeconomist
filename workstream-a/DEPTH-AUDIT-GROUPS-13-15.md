# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 13–15

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 13–15 in the Workstream A roadmap against Concepts 20, 21 and 23 and relevant connected notes.  
**Purpose:** Confirm conceptual coverage, make targeted improvements where a consequential explanatory gap exists, and separate conceptual work from formal measurement and jurisdiction-specific verification. This audit does not validate current company filings, Nigerian legal rules or current risk statistics.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and its principal boundaries.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears, but an important mechanism or distinction needs clearer treatment.
- **Cross-linked:** Another note is the primary home for the topic; link rather than duplicate it.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed notes.

A concept note can explain a mechanism without establishing the current facts of a particular company, institution, law, market or event.

## 2. Group 13 — Business structure, ownership and corporate finance

| Equity, shares, dilution and shareholder claims | Explicitly covered | Concept 20 covers share capital, equity claims and ownership changes. A precise dilution calculation must state the pre/post-money basis, share classes, options and transaction terms. |

### Group 13 conclusion

Group 13 is conceptually covered, with a targeted addition on revenue models, margins, operating leverage and unit economics. Remaining work is case-specific: reconcile the entity boundary, source of revenue, cost allocation, customer/payment terms, financing obligations and the period used. Unit economics should not be presented as a universal standardized metric unless its unit and included costs are defined.

## 3. Group 14 — Risk, uncertainty and financial resilience

**Primary note:** [Concept 21 — Risk, Uncertainty and Financial Resilience](concepts/risk-uncertainty-and-financial-resilience.md). Related notes include Concepts 10, 17–19, 20, 24 and 25.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Risk versus uncertainty | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 distinguishes measurable risk from situations where probabilities, outcomes or the model are poorly known. |
| Volatility, drawdown and tail risk | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 separates routine variation from loss severity and tail exposure. Results depend on horizon, data, distribution and metric. |
| Credit, market, liquidity, operational and counterparty risk | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 covers these risk families and their different transmission mechanisms; the categories can overlap in a real event. |
| Concentration, contagion and correlated exposures | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 explains concentration and stress propagation. Correlation measured in ordinary periods may not describe behavior during a systemic shock. |
| Solvency, liquidity and ability to meet obligations | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 distinguishes ability to meet near-term payments from the longer-run relationship between assets, liabilities and loss-absorbing capacity. |
| Insurance, hedging, guarantees and risk transfer | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 explains contractual risk transfer and residual risks such as exclusions, counterparty capacity, basis risk and moral hazard. |
| Stress testing, scenarios and sensitivity analysis | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 covers scenario analysis and early-warning indicators. Results are conditional on scenario choice, data and model assumptions, not predictions. |
| Reserves, buffers and resilience | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 covers usable buffers, redundancy, optionality and recovery capacity; nominal resources may be inaccessible or impaired during stress. |
| Default, recovery, loss given default and expected loss | **Core measures covered; recovery assumptions strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 21 already explains expected loss and LGD. The new subsection emphasizes collateral, enforceability, priority, recovery timing, costs and the danger of transferring a recovery rate across contexts without validation. |
| Systemic risk and feedback amplification | Explicitly covered | Concept 21 covers institution-specific versus systemic risk and feedback loops. Evidence of a single institution's distress does not by itself establish system-wide contagion. |

### Group 14 conclusion

Group 14 is conceptually covered, with a targeted addition distinguishing risk appetite, tolerance, limits, capacity and recovery assumptions. Remaining work is to define metrics, horizons, triggers, scenario severity, escalation ownership and response actions for the particular organization or system. A risk policy is not operational unless thresholds, accountability and responses are specified.

## 4. Group 15 — Economic systems, institutions and structural change

**Primary note:** [Concept 23 — Economic Systems, Institutions and Structural Change](concepts/economic-systems-institutions-and-structural-change.md). Related notes include Concepts 12, 15, 20, 22, 24, 26 and 31.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Economic systems and institutional arrangements | Explicitly covered | Concept 23 treats systems as combinations of ownership, coordination, allocation, finance, distribution and accountability rather than a single capitalism/socialism label. |
| Property rights, contracts, enforcement and trust | **Mechanisms strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 23 now distinguishes formal rights from practical ability to exercise them, and explains courts, administrative remedies, reputation, collateral and repeated dealings as possible enforcement mechanisms. |
| Formal and informal institutions | Explicitly covered | Concept 23 distinguishes laws and organizations from norms, customs, trust and informal practices, including cases where they reinforce or conflict with one another. |
| Incentives, power, bargaining and governance | Explicitly covered | Concept 23 connects institutional arrangements to constraints, decisions and the distribution of control, benefits and risks. A specific power claim needs evidence of actual control or bargaining conditions. |
| Regulation, deregulation and public policy | **Mechanisms strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 23 now explains that deregulation does not necessarily mean absence of rules and that effects depend on the particular rule, implementation, market structure and participant response. |
| Structural transformation and sectoral reallocation | Explicitly covered | Concept 23 explains structural change across sectors, technologies, organization and institutions; Concept 26 is the related home for growth/development and productivity distinctions. |
| Industrialization, deindustrialization and economic complexity | Explicitly covered conceptually / cross-linked | Concept 23 defines industrialization and related changes; Concept 26 develops growth and structural transformation. Quantitative complexity claims need explicit methods and data. |
| Path dependence, lock-in and institutional change | Explicitly covered | Concept 23 covers persistence, sunk costs, accumulated capabilities, expectations and transition costs. A historical sequence alone does not prove causal lock-in. |
| Political economy and distribution of decision-making power | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concept 23 explains economic power and governance; Concept 31 provides a more focused treatment of power and opportunity. |
| Rules, norms, networks and collective action | **Collective-action mechanism strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 23 now explains coordination problems, free-riding, trust, cost-sharing, monitoring, sanctions and legitimacy as factors that shape collective action. |

### Group 15 conclusion

Group 15 is conceptually covered, with targeted additions on property rights and enforcement, regulation/deregulation, and collective action. Remaining work is empirical and jurisdiction-specific: establish the applicable legal text, responsible authority, implementation capacity, actual practice, distributional effects and alternative explanations. A written rule is not evidence by itself that rights are enforceable or outcomes improved.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 20 — Business Structure, Ownership and Corporate Finance:** Added “Revenue models, margins, operating leverage and unit economics.” It distinguishes revenue model from broader business model, margin measures, fixed/variable costs, contribution per unit and whole-business profitability/cash outcomes.
2. **Concept 21 — Risk, Uncertainty and Financial Resilience:** Added “Risk appetite, tolerance, limits and recovery.” It separates willingness to take risk from the capacity to absorb it and adds recovery assumptions to LGD analysis.
3. **Concept 23 — Economic Systems, Institutions and Structural Change:** Added “Property rights, contract enforcement, regulation and collective action.” It clarifies formal rights versus practical enforcement, regulation versus deregulation, and the coordination barriers to collective action.

No current Nigerian corporate filings, legal interpretations, risk levels or institutional-effectiveness claims were added or asserted as verified.

## 6. Remaining measurement and evidence work

- **Business and ownership:** Verify the legal entity, ownership/beneficial-ownership basis, share classes, corporate filings, accounting period and transaction status. For unit economics, specify the unit, costs included, revenue-recognition basis and cash-collection assumptions.
- **Risk:** Define exposure, probability model, horizon, loss metric, stress scenario, recovery assumptions, thresholds, limit owner and response procedure. Validate whether buffers remain usable under the scenario.
- **Institutions and structural change:** Use jurisdiction- and date-specific legal texts, implementation records, budgets, enforcement decisions, observed behavior and outcome data. Keep stated policy, implementation, causal inference and distributional judgement separate.
- **Evidence discipline:** Distinguish a concept, a plausible mechanism, a measured association, a causal finding and a legal conclusion. Record uncertainty and disconfirming evidence.

## 7. Audit decision and next step

Groups 13–15 are **conceptually covered**, with targeted additions made to Concepts 20, 21 and 23. The remaining work is primarily formal measurement, current-source verification and application to specific organizations or institutional settings. No new numbered concept is justified by this audit.

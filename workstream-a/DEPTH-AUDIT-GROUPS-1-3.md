# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 1–3

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 1–3 in the Workstream A roadmap, using the actual content of Concepts 1–11.  
**Purpose:** Separate explicit coverage, partial coverage, cross-links and missing treatment. This is a conceptual coverage audit, not empirical validation or a formal technical certification.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The relevant concept note has a direct explanation and the main boundary is stated.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears, but a material distinction or mechanism still needs more precise treatment.
- **Cross-linked:** The topic is handled principally in another concept; the roadmap item should point to that note rather than duplicate it.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the notes reviewed.

“Explicitly covered” means covered at the current conceptual stage. It does not mean every technical sub-question is settled or that a country-specific statistic has been validated.

## 2. Group 1 — Production and creation of economic value

**Primary notes:** Concepts 1–8.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Production and productive activity | Explicitly covered | Concept 1 distinguishes production from effort, payment and usefulness; it treats goods and services and identifies the GDP production boundary. |
| Goods, services, output and intermediate inputs | Explicitly covered | Concepts 1–2 explain outputs, inputs, intermediate consumption and why output differs from sales and cash receipts. |
| Production processes and transformation | Explicitly covered | Concepts 1–2 explain how inputs are combined or transformed into outputs; examples include production and service activities. |
| Usefulness, economic value, exchange value and welfare | Explicitly covered | Concept 3 separates usefulness, willingness to pay, transaction price, accounting exchange values and welfare effects, including externalities and double-counting risks. |
| Intermediate consumption and value added | Explicitly covered | Concepts 2 and 6 define intermediate consumption and value added; Concept 6 connects firm-level value added with national accounting. |
| Costs, prices, revenue, income and profit | Explicitly covered | Concepts 4–6 define these terms and show that they answer different questions. Concept 5 covers revenue recognition, receipts and cash timing; Concept 6 separates value added from business profit. |
| Productivity, labour productivity and total-factor productivity | **Partially covered / cross-linked** | Concept 7 explains productivity and identifies labour and multifactor productivity, including the model dependence of the multifactor residual. Concept 14 and Concept 26 add labour/aggregate context. A formal TFP specification and its assumptions belong in measurement work, not a generic concept definition. |
| Capacity, capacity utilization, efficiency and technical change | **Partially covered / cross-linked** | Concept 7 discusses productive capacity, capacity utilization and drivers such as tools, skills, infrastructure, management and technical change. A rigorous utilization rate, potential-output estimate or technical-change attribution is not yet a formal operational measure. |
| Capital formation, maintenance, depreciation and replacement | Explicitly covered | Concept 8 distinguishes gross/net capital formation, accounting depreciation versus consumption of fixed capital, maintenance, replacement and expansion; it cautions that asset acquisition does not prove productive use or improved outcomes. |

### Group 1 conclusion

Group 1 has strong conceptual coverage across Concepts 1–8. The remaining gaps are chiefly formalization and measurement: inventory/work-in-progress adjustments and timing need consistent application in actual datasets; total-factor productivity and capacity utilization need explicit operational definitions and suitable data before quantitative claims. These should be handled in the relevant note or Workstream B specification, not by creating another broad production concept.

## 3. Group 2 — Money, wealth, capital and financial resources

**Primary note:** Concept 9 — Money, Wealth, Capital and Financial Resources; related notes include Concepts 6, 8, 10, 11, 13, 19 and 24.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Money, currency, cash and monetary aggregates | **Explicitly covered after this audit's targeted addition** | Concept 9 already distinguished currency, cash, deposits, payment instruments and reserves. This audit adds a section explaining monetary aggregates as definition-dependent statistical groupings and requires source metadata for country-specific series. |
| Bank deposits, central-bank reserves and physical currency | Explicitly covered | Concept 9 distinguishes the depositor's claim on a bank from reserve balances held by eligible institutions at the central bank and explains payment/settlement at a conceptual level. |
| Wealth, net worth and ownership of assets | Explicitly covered | Concept 9 defines wealth/net worth as a boundary- and valuation-dependent stock and illustrates assets less liabilities. |
| Assets, liabilities and equity/net worth | Explicitly covered | Concept 9 explains assets and liabilities and shows how a loan increases cash/deposits and liabilities without automatically increasing net worth. Concept 19 covers financial statements in greater detail. |
| Capital in economic theory, accounting and finance | Explicitly covered | Concept 9 separates productive/real, financial, accounting/regulatory, human, social and natural-capital usages and warns that context must be named. |
| Physical/produced capital, human capital, natural capital and financial capital | Explicitly covered | Concepts 8–9 distinguish produced assets from human capabilities, natural resources and financial claims. These categories should not be assumed to be valued or recorded in the same way. |
| Income, earnings, receipts and cash flow | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concepts 5, 6 and 9 separate income/revenue recognition, receipts, profit, cash flow and value added. Concept 5 is the detailed reference for timing and accounting treatment. |
| Savings, liquidity and purchasing power | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concepts 9, 11 and 16 treat saving, liquidity and purchasing power in distinct contexts. Saving is a flow; liquidity is ability to meet payments or convert assets; purchasing power depends on prices and the relevant basket. |
| Valuation, market value and book value | Explicitly covered | Concepts 3, 8, 9 and 19 explain that valuation basis and date matter; book value is not automatically market or replacement value. |
| Stocks versus flows; gross versus net measures | Explicitly covered / cross-linked | Concepts 6, 8, 9, 10 and 32 address these distinctions. The correct gross/net deduction and stock/flow period must be named for each statistic. |

### Group 2 conclusion

The main conceptual boundaries are covered. The targeted addition to Concept 9 makes monetary aggregates more explicit and strengthens the separation between currency, deposits and central-bank reserves. A remaining implementation task is to verify the current Central Bank of Nigeria definitions and metadata if Lifeconomist builds Nigeria-specific money indicators. This audit does not assert a current monetary aggregate value or a particular CBN series definition.

## 4. Group 3 — Credit, debt, banking and financial intermediation

**Primary note:** Concept 10 — Credit, Debt, Banking and Financial Intermediation; related notes include Concepts 9, 18, 19, 21, 24 and 32.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Credit, lending, borrowing and repayment | Explicitly covered | Concept 10 defines the roles and describes loan transactions and balance-sheet changes. It also recognizes non-bank credit such as supplier credit. |
| Debt, principal, interest and debt service | Explicitly covered | Concept 10 separates debt stock, borrowing/repayment flows, interest costs and debt-service measures. |
| Loans, bonds, deposits and other claims | Explicitly covered | Concept 10 discusses loan claims, bond issuance and deposits; Concept 19 gives the broader balance-sheet/accounting treatment. The instruments have different legal and balance-sheet roles. |
| Banks, non-bank lenders and financial intermediaries | Explicitly covered | Concept 10 describes intermediary functions and includes banks, non-bank lenders and supplier credit. Institutional and jurisdiction-specific arrangements still need local evidence. |
| Deposit creation and bank balance sheets | Explicitly covered | Concept 10 explains how bank lending can create deposits, how loans and deposits are recorded, and why credit creation does not automatically create net wealth. |
| Central banks, monetary base and reserve systems | **Partially covered / cross-linked** | Concept 10 distinguishes central-bank reserves and deposit money; Concept 9 now clarifies the monetary aggregate boundary. A complete operational account of a specific central-bank framework, reserve regime or monetary-base series must be tied to the jurisdiction and period. |
| Collateral, guarantees, secured and unsecured credit | **Partially covered / cross-linked** | Concept 10 covers collateral, guarantees in sustainability checks, secured-loan limitations and underwriting. A fuller taxonomy of guarantees, enforceability, priority, collateral valuation and contingent liabilities may be needed for a particular transaction or jurisdiction. Concept 32 also distinguishes direct debt from contingent liabilities. |
| Creditworthiness, default, arrears and loss | **Partially covered** | Concept 10 covers creditworthiness, default, underwriting, collateral and loss. It flags arrears in the measurement checklist but does not yet fully distinguish days-past-due buckets, non-performing classifications, impairment/expected credit loss and realized loss. Those definitions vary by contract, accounting standard, regulator and dataset. |
| Leverage, maturity transformation and maturity mismatch | **Explicitly covered after this audit's targeted addition** | Concept 10 defines leverage and sustainability; the new subsection distinguishes maturity transformation, maturity mismatch and liquidity risk. |
| Refinancing, rollover risk, restructuring and deleveraging | **Explicitly covered after this audit's targeted addition, with context-dependent measurement remaining** | The new Concept 10 subsection distinguishes refinancing risk, rollover risk, repricing risk, arrears, restructuring and deleveraging. Quantitative stress tests still require entity-specific maturity schedules, cash-flow assumptions and funding data. |

### Group 3 conclusion

The core lending and balance-sheet mechanism is already explained. This audit adds the most consequential missing connective tissue: maturity transformation, mismatch, rollover/refinancing risk, repricing, arrears, restructuring and deleveraging. The largest remaining technical gap is the context-specific classification of credit impairment, non-performing exposures, guarantees and contingent liabilities. Those should be specified when Lifeconomist investigates a particular bank, borrower, jurisdiction or dataset rather than treated as universal categories.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 9:** Added “Monetary aggregates: why the boundary matters.” It explains that aggregate labels are definition-dependent, separates public currency, eligible bank deposits and central-bank reserves, cautions against a mechanical reserve-to-lending relationship, and specifies the metadata required for a Nigeria-specific measure.
2. **Concept 10:** Added “Maturity transformation, mismatch and rollover risk.” It defines maturity transformation, maturity mismatch, liquidity risk, rollover risk, refinancing risk, repricing risk, arrears, restructuring and deleveraging; explains the mechanism in plain language; and supplies an eight-point investigation checklist.

These additions fill identifiable explanatory gaps without creating duplicate numbered concepts. They do not claim that current Nigerian monetary aggregates, bank classifications or credit-risk measures have been verified.

## 6. Cross-cutting findings and next work

- **Inventory and timing:** Concepts 1–2 explain the issue, but data applications must handle work-in-progress and inventory changes under the selected accounting framework and period.
- **Multifactor productivity and capacity utilization:** The concepts establish the meaning and limitations. Any indicator needs a formal output/input definition, source, time basis, coverage and model assumptions.
- **Monetary aggregates:** Use current central-bank series definitions and metadata before producing country-level measurements.
- **Credit-risk categories:** Specify the contract, regulator, accounting framework and reference dataset before equating arrears, default, non-performing exposure and impairment.
- **Collateral and guarantees:** Distinguish legal obligation, contingent exposure, collateral value, enforceability and realized recovery.
- **No Concept 33 yet:** The reviewed groups do not currently justify a new broad concept. First complete the same depth audit for the next groups or resolve a concrete gap in an existing note.

## 7. Audit decision

Groups 1–3 are **conceptually covered, with targeted additions made where a material explanatory gap was identified**. The principal remaining work is precise measurement and context-specific classification, not a new general-purpose concept. Keep the existing note architecture, link overlapping topics, and create new numbered concepts only for distinct gaps that cannot be handled more coherently inside the current notes.

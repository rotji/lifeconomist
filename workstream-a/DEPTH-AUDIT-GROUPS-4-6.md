# Depth Audit — Workstream A Groups 4–6

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Bullet-by-bullet review of Groups 4–6 in the Workstream A roadmap, against Concepts 11–13 and relevant connected notes.  
**Purpose:** Separate conceptual coverage from explanatory gaps and formal measurement work. This is a documentation audit, not empirical validation of current market, investment or Nigerian national-account data.

## 1. Status definitions

- **Explicitly covered:** The note directly explains the topic and the main conceptual boundary.
- **Partially covered:** The topic appears, but an important mechanism, distinction or practical implication needs clearer treatment.
- **Cross-linked:** Another concept is the appropriate main treatment; the current note should point to it rather than duplicate it.
- **Not yet covered:** No adequate treatment was found in the reviewed material.

A conceptual note can be explicit while a quantitative indicator remains unspecified. This audit does not treat an explanation as a substitute for formal measurement definitions, current source metadata or legal/regulatory verification.

## 2. Group 4 — Investment, savings and capital allocation

**Primary note:** [Concept 11 — Investment, Saving, Financial Markets and Capital Allocation](concepts/investment-saving-financial-markets-and-capital-allocation.md). Relevant cross-links include Concepts 7–9, 13, 18 and 25.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Saving versus investment | Explicitly covered | Concept 11 distinguishes saving as a flow from accumulated savings as a stock and separates financial and national-accounting meanings of investment. |
| Investment expenditure versus financial-asset purchases | Explicitly covered | Concept 11 explains that financial claims can change hands without new productive assets being created; Concept 13 covers capital formation in national accounts. |
| Productive investment and capital formation | Explicitly covered | Concepts 8, 11 and 13 distinguish capital formation, financial transactions, maintenance and actual productive use. |
| Portfolio allocation, reallocation and diversification | Explicitly covered after targeted addition | Concept 11 now distinguishes allocation, reallocation and diversification, including why a larger number of holdings does not guarantee distinct risk exposures. |
| Capital expenditure (CapEx) and operating expenditure (OpEx) | Explicitly covered after targeted addition, with accounting-policy limits | Concept 11 now explains their general meanings and warns that classification depends on applicable accounting rules, asset/useful-life criteria and policy—not just an invoice label. |
| Expected return, realized return and total return | Explicitly covered after targeted addition | Concept 11 now separates forecasts from outcomes and explains that total-return comparisons need a defined period and treatment of income, fees, taxes, currency and reinvestment. |
| Investment horizon, reinvestment and compounding | Explicitly covered | Concept 11 already explains time horizon and compounding; returns require a consistent time basis. |
| Speculation, hedging and arbitrage | Explicitly covered after targeted addition | Concept 11 now distinguishes risk-taking for expected gain, offsetting an existing exposure and exploiting price inconsistencies after execution and funding costs. A displayed price gap is not proof of executable arbitrage. |
| Commitments, pledges, approvals, disbursements and deployed capital | Explicitly covered | Concept 11 separates announcement/commitment stages from transfer, deployment, completion and outcomes; Concept 25 supplies related fund-specific stages. |

A claim of efficient allocation still needs a stated objective, feasible alternatives, costs, risk and defensible outcome measures. |

### Group 4 conclusion

Group 4 is conceptually covered. The targeted additions strengthen portfolio-strategy vocabulary and the distinction between planned returns, realized results and transaction labels. Formal performance comparisons still require a declared benchmark, time period, cash-flow treatment, fees, taxes, inflation/currency assumptions and risk basis. Those belong in the relevant analysis or Workstream B specification rather than a new general concept.

### Group 5 conclusion

Group 5 is conceptually covered, with a useful expansion on market access, information asymmetry and bargaining. The remaining work is application-specific: comparable price data, reliable quantity/cost information, market definition, entry/exit evidence, contracts, and executable bid/ask prices where financial-market claims are made. Market power and arbitrage claims require stronger evidence than a price difference or headline market share.

## 4. Group 6 — National income, GDP and economic accounting

**Primary note:** [Concept 13 — National Income, GDP and Economic Accounting](concepts/national-income-gdp-and-economic-accounting.md). Relevant cross-links include Concepts 6, 8, 14–16, 19, 26 and 29.

| Roadmap bullet | Status | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| GDP, gross value added and national output | Explicitly covered | Concept 13 explains the production boundary, GVA, adjustments and the relationship to GDP. |
| Nominal versus real GDP and growth | Explicitly covered | Concept 13 distinguishes current-price values from volume measures and cautions that index-number methods affect exact comparisons. |
| GDP deflator and consumer-price indices | Explicitly covered after targeted addition | Concept 13 now compares coverage and purpose: the GDP deflator relates domestic production to its volume measure, while CPI tracks a defined consumer basket. Neither is a universal measure of all prices. |
| Production, expenditure and income approaches to GDP | Explicitly covered | Concept 13 explains the three approaches and their conceptual relationship; the practical compilation of a country's estimates requires official methods and data. |
| Final goods, intermediate goods and double counting | Explicitly covered | Concepts 2, 6 and 13 distinguish final output, intermediate consumption and value added to avoid counting the same production repeatedly. |
| Gross national income and national disposable income | **GNI explicitly covered; national disposable income strengthened by targeted addition** | Concept 13 already explains GNI and cross-border primary income. It now explains how net current transfers enter national disposable income and why neither national total equals the disposable income of a typical household. |
| Consumption, investment, government spending and net exports | Explicitly covered | Concept 13 explains the expenditure approach and relevant components; classification and source definitions matter in an operational series. |
| Sectoral output, GDP shares and contributions to growth | Explicitly covered | Concept 13 distinguishes sector size, sector growth and contribution to aggregate growth, and warns that nominal shares can change with relative prices. |
| National wealth versus annual production/income | Explicitly covered | Concepts 9 and 13 distinguish stocks such as wealth from flows such as GDP and income. |
| Informal activity, imputed output and non-market production boundaries | Explicitly covered conceptually; actual coverage depends on compilation | Concept 13 explains that informal or illegal activity is not automatically outside the conceptual production boundary, and that non-market and imputed items can be included under defined rules. The extent actually captured in a country's estimates must be verified. |

### Group 6 conclusion

Group 6 is conceptually covered. The additions clarify the GDP deflator/CPI distinction and the boundary between GDP, GNI, national disposable income and household disposable income. The largest remaining task is measurement-specific: establish the current Nigerian national-accounts framework, base/reference year, series, sector classification, deflators, source tables, release vintage, revision policy and calculation rules from the National Bureau of Statistics. This audit does not assert that any current Nigerian GDP figure or compilation method has been independently verified.

## 5. Targeted changes made during this audit

1. **Concept 11 — Investment, Saving, Financial Markets and Capital Allocation:** Added three subsections on (a) CapEx/OpEx and decision quality, expected/realized/total return and compounding; (b) portfolio allocation, diversification and reallocation; and (c) speculation, hedging and arbitrage. The additions identify classification and execution limitations.
2. **Concept 12 — Markets, Prices and Exchange:** Added a subsection on market access, information asymmetry, bargaining power and barriers to entry, including evidence requirements and the distinction between a plausible mechanism and a legal/causal finding.
3. **Concept 13 — National Income, GDP and Economic Accounting:** Added subsections comparing the GDP deflator with CPI and explaining national disposable income relative to GNI, GDP and household disposable income.

No current Nigerian investment, market-price or GDP statistics were added. The changes are conceptual clarifications, not new empirical findings.

## 6. Remaining measurement and evidence work

- **Investment performance:** Choose a benchmark and time horizon; specify treatment of fees, taxes, cash flows, inflation, currency and unrealized gains.
- **CapEx/OpEx:** Apply the relevant accounting standard and entity policy to the actual transaction; do not classify solely by everyday wording.
- **Market structure:** Define the relevant product/geographic market and use multiple forms of evidence before inferring market power or barriers.
- **Arbitrage:** Verify time-aligned executable bid/ask quotes, size, access, fees, funding, settlement, legal conditions and operational risk.
- **Nigeria national accounts:** Verify NBS methodology and metadata directly before writing a formal Workstream B GDP indicator or making a current-data claim.
- **CPI versus GDP deflator:** Select the index that matches the question; record coverage, weights, construction, reference period and revision rules.
- **National income distribution:** Use sector/household distribution data if the question concerns who benefits; aggregate GDP or national disposable income does not reveal a typical household's outcome.

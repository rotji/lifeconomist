# Depth Audit — Workstream A Group 25 / Concept 32

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Scope:** Audit every required comparison listed under Group 25 in the Workstream A roadmap against Concept 32, the Commonly Confused Economic and Financial Terms: Comparison Register.  
**Purpose:** Verify explicit coverage, identify maintenance gaps, and strengthen traceability without duplicating the detailed concept notes or replacing context-specific standards.

## 1. Assessment method

A comparison counts as covered only when the register explains the relevant distinction and why it matters. Merely mentioning both terms is insufficient. The register is conceptual and cross-referential: accounting, legal, market and national-accounting questions still require the applicable standard, jurisdiction, unit, time period and entity boundary.

Status labels:
- **Covered:** The distinction is explicitly explained in the existing register.
- **Covered; traceability improved:** The distinction was already explained, and a checklist row was added so the roadmap requirement can be audited directly.
- **Context-dependent:** The register correctly flags that definitions vary; formal work must state the selected standard or convention.

## 3. Targeted change made

The existing Concept 32 already had substantial coverage of all required comparisons. The main gap was **auditability and traceability**: the roadmap's list of required comparisons was not mirrored in a single checklist inside the concept note. That made it harder to verify completeness or maintain the register when future concept work introduces a new ambiguity.

I added **“2.1 Roadmap coverage checklist”** to Concept 32. It contains a row for every required roadmap comparison, points to the relevant section, and states the core test to apply. It also records an explicit rule: a pair is not covered merely because both terms appear; the distinction must identify a boundary and explain why confusion matters.

No wholesale rewrite was warranted. The existing note already included the explanations, examples and links needed for this group. The change preserves its role as a cross-reference layer and avoids duplicating detailed concepts.

## 4. Remaining limitations and implementation tasks

- **Context-specific definitions:** Capital, income, solvency, funding, commitment, deployment, value and cash generated can vary by discipline or reporting framework. Formal analysis must name the definition and standard.
- **National accounts:** GDP, value added, real growth, sector contributions and gross/net measures require consistent statistical definitions, price methods, periods and classifications.
- **Accounting:** Revenue, profit, cash flow, book value and receipts must be interpreted under the applicable accounting policy and reporting period.
- **Public finance and programmes:** Budget stages and fiscal quantities require jurisdiction-specific legal and accounting definitions; an announcement does not establish a completed transaction or outcome.
- **Financial markets:** Market value, liquidity, reserves, deposits, ownership and settlement depend on the instrument, institution and market rules.
- **Data normalization:** When collecting evidence, preserve the source's original wording and record the normalized concept separately. Do not silently replace a source's definition with Lifeconomist's preferred label.

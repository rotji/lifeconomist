Concept 8, [Capital Formation, Depreciation, Maintenance and Replacement](workstream-a/concepts/capital-formation-depreciation-maintenance-and-replacement.md), is now documented. It distinguishes capital formation from financial investment, saving, borrowing and transfers of existing assets; separates gross from net measures; and explains depreciation versus national-accounts consumption of fixed capital, maintenance and productive capacity. Concept 9, [Money, Wealth, Capital and Financial Resources](workstream-a/concepts/money-wealth-capital-and-financial-resources.md), is now documented. It separates money and deposits from wealth/net worth, income and saving; distinguishes productive, financial and regulatory capital; and explains assets/liabilities, financing, liquidity and solvency. Concept 10, [Credit, Debt, Banking and Financial Intermediation](workstream-a/concepts/credit-debt-banking-and-financial-intermediation.md), is now documented. It distinguishes credit arrangements from debt obligations, debt stocks from borrowing and repayment flows, principal from interest, deposits from central-bank reserves, and commercial-bank lending from the outcomes of credit allocation. Concept 11, [Investment, Saving, Financial Markets and Capital Allocation](workstream-a/concepts/investment-saving-financial-markets-and-capital-allocation.md), is now documented. It distinguishes household saving from accumulated savings, financial investment from national-accounting capital formation, primary issuance from secondary trading, and investment commitments from disbursement, deployment and outcomes. Concept 12, [Markets, Prices and Exchange](workstream-a/concepts/markets-prices-and-exchange.md), is now documented. It distinguishes market from marketplace, price from cost and value, nominal from real prices, supply from quantity supplied, demand from quantity demanded, model equilibrium from fairness, and financial-market repricing from productive capacity. Concept 13, [National Income, GDP and Economic Accounting](workstream-a/concepts/national-income-gdp-and-economic-accounting.md), is now documented. It distinguishes GDP from wealth, money stocks, GNI and household income; explains final versus intermediate output, value added, the production/expenditure/income approaches, nominal versus real GDP, and GDP's welfare limitations. Workstream B retains responsibility for formal GDP indicator definitions, source selection and calculation rules. Concept 14, [Labour, Employment and Household Economics](workstream-a/concepts/labour-employment-and-household-economics.md), is now documented. It distinguishes employment, unemployment, underemployment, participation and employment-to-population ratios; wages, earnings and compensation; nominal versus real pay; household income versus disposable income; consumption, saving, wealth, poverty, informality and productivity. Workstream B retains responsibility for official Nigerian series and formal indicator specifications. Concept 15, [Government Finance, Taxation and Public Institutions](workstream-a/concepts/government-finance-taxation-and-public-institutions.md), is now documented. It distinguishes revenue from financing; tax liability from tax incidence; budget authorization, commitment and payment; deficit from debt; direct debt from contingent fiscal risks; and spending inputs from public-service outcomes. It also covers fiscal boundaries, cash/liquidity, arrears, intergovernmental transfers and transparency. No current Nigerian fiscal figures are asserted; Workstream B retains responsibility for official series and formal indicator specifications. Concept 16, [Inflation, Interest Rates and Purchasing Power](workstream-a/concepts/inflation-interest-rates-and-purchasing-power.md), is now documented. It distinguishes price level from inflation rate, disinflation from deflation, CPI from a full cost-of-living concept, nominal from real income and interest rates, expected from realized inflation, and policy rates from market rates. It also covers index limitations, potential inflation mechanisms, household distributional effects and causal-evidence safeguards. No current Nigerian inflation or interest-rate figures are asserted; Workstream B retains responsibility for official series and formal indicator specifications. Concept 17, [Foreign Exchange, International Money and Global Capital Flows](workstream-a/concepts/foreign-exchange-international-money-and-global-capital-flows.md), is now documented. It distinguishes exchange-rate quotations from purchasing power; bilateral from effective exchange-rate measures; appreciation/depreciation from official parity changes; spot from forward rates; currency denomination from residence and ownership; gross/net flows from stocks; and transactions from valuation changes. It also covers balance-of-payments accounts, the international investment position, investment categories, reserve definitions, exchange-rate pass-through and currency mismatch. No current Nigerian FX, reserve, debt or capital-flow values are asserted; Workstream B retains responsibility for official series and formal indicator specifications. ## ECAI — Economic Consequence and Agency Intelligence

- **Status:** Provisional research framework documented in `ECAI-FOUNDATION.md`; not validated as a unified method, product, or intervention system.
- **Purpose:** Connect analysis of economic consequences with evidence-based assessment of what individuals and groups can realistically influence.
- **Architecture:** Cross-cutting application of existing Workstreams A–E and the Reality-First methodology, not a new workstream and not a replacement for the Global Financial System Exploration Map.
- **Current stage:** Stage 1 — foundational literature synthesis in progress. [ECAI-LITERATURE-SYNTHESIS-001.md](ECAI-LITERATURE-SYNTHESIS-001.md) records the initial source-backed synthesis across consumer welfare, political consumerism, boycotts, collective action, production networks, and evaluation. This is a selective initial synthesis, not a systematic review.
- **Not established:** A complete literature review, validated consequence-tracing protocol, comparative case register, or preferred practical application.
- **Initial synthesis findings:** Keep participation, market effects, institutional response, social outcomes, and durability separate; distinguish boycotts from buycotts; match supply-chain claims to data resolution; assess unintended and distributional effects; treat collective-action principles as context-dependent.
- **Next action:** Expand and verify the literature, then specify a repeatable consequence-tracing methodology and examine documented cases before selecting an implementation direction.

### Workstream E — FX programme
The initial FX programme investigates mathematical currency relationships, economic connections, information timing, market access and conversion friction, fully funded currency rotation, broker-based FX products as a separate track, and potential FX intelligence products/services. Global scope and Nigeria-specific access questions are both in scope. Historical/public data, manual calculations, spreadsheets and simulation come before live trading. The available $20 is not to be risked as part of the initial research. See `workstream-e/fx/FX-OPPORTUNITY-INTELLIGENCE-PROGRAMME.md` for the living specification and first practical task.

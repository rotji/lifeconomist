# Global Financial Assets and Liabilities: Accounting Foundations

**Status:** Conceptual accounting foundation; measurement design and empirical validation pending.  
**Started:** 2026-11-13  
**Parent:** [Global Financial System Exploration Map](GLOBAL-FINANCIAL-SYSTEM-EXPLORATION-MAP.md)

## 1. Purpose and boundary

This note establishes the accounting logic needed before attempting to estimate global financial assets and liabilities. It is not an estimate of their total value, a complete global balance sheet, or a claim that all countries and sectors are covered by one consistent dataset.

The central question is:

**For each financial asset, what claim does it represent, who holds the claim, who is the counterpart issuer or debtor, and how should both sides be represented when moving from individual balance sheets to sector, country, and global views?**

The unit of analysis is a balance-sheet position at a point in time unless explicitly identified as a transaction or other flow over a period.

## 2. The basic balance-sheet relationship

A balance sheet records assets and liabilities at a specified date and under a specified valuation basis.

For an institutional unit:

**Net worth = Total assets − Total liabilities.**

Assets can include:
- **Non-financial assets:** produced assets (buildings, machinery, infrastructure, inventories) and non-produced assets (such as land and certain natural resources), subject to the accounting framework.
- **Financial assets:** cash, deposits, debt securities, loans, equity and investment-fund shares, insurance and pension entitlements, financial derivatives, and other financial claims recognised by the applicable framework.

Liabilities are obligations or counterpart claims recorded against the unit. Financial liabilities commonly include deposits issued, loans payable, debt securities issued, equity and investment-fund shares issued, insurance/pension obligations, derivatives with a liability value, and other accounts payable.

A balance-sheet position is a **stock** at a date. A loan advanced during a quarter is a **transaction/flow** during that quarter. The transaction changes positions, but the closing stock can also change because of valuation movements, exchange rates, reclassifications, write-offs, and other volume changes.

## 3. What makes an asset financial?

A financial asset is generally a financial claim or a recognised form of monetary gold under the relevant statistical framework. Most financial assets have a counterpart liability: one unit holds an asset and another unit records the corresponding liability.

Examples:

| Holder's financial asset | Counterpart issuer / debtor liability |
|---|---|
| Bank deposit held by a household | Deposit liability of the bank |
| Government bond held by a pension fund | Debt-security liability of the government |
| Bank loan held by a bank | Loan liability of the household or business borrower |
| Corporate bond held by an insurer | Debt-security liability of the issuing company |
| Equity share held by an investor | Equity issued by the corporation |
| Derivative with positive value to one counterparty | Corresponding negative-value position for the other counterparty, subject to applicable valuation and reporting rules |
| Pension entitlement of a household | Pension liability of the responsible pension arrangement/employer/government, depending on the scheme and accounting treatment |

**Plain-language meaning:** A financial asset is often not a separate physical object that exists independently on both sides. It is a claim held by one party and an obligation or ownership claim recorded by another. The holder's asset and the counterpart's liability describe the same financial relationship from opposite sides.

The table is schematic. Instrument classification, institutional boundaries, valuation, and the treatment of equity, pensions, derivatives, and monetary gold must follow the specific statistical framework used.

## 4. Why gross financial assets and liabilities cannot simply be added

Suppose a household has a bank deposit of 100 monetary units. The household records a deposit asset of 100. The bank records a deposit liability of 100.

If both balance sheets are added together:
- Household financial assets include 100.
- Bank financial liabilities include 100.

These are not two independent pieces of global wealth. They are the two sides of one claim.

The same issue appears throughout the system:
- A bank loan is an asset of the lender and a liability of the borrower.
- A government bond is an asset of its holder and a liability of the issuing government.
- A corporate share is an asset of the investor and an equity claim issued by the company.

When consolidating the relevant units, matched intra-boundary financial claims and liabilities cancel. This is why a sum of every institution's reported financial assets is not a measure of additional net global wealth.

However, gross positions remain important. They show who owes whom, the scale of intermediation, leverage, funding dependence, maturity and currency mismatches, concentration, and potential transmission of distress. Cancellation in a consolidated accounting view does not make the underlying relationships economically unimportant.

## 5. Consolidation: the boundary changes the answer

Consolidation means combining units within a defined boundary and eliminating financial positions between units inside that boundary. The boundary must always be stated.

### 5.1 Individual institution

Record all assets and liabilities of one bank, household, company, fund, or government unit. Claims on other units remain visible.

### 5.2 Institutional sector

Combine units classified in a sector (for example, households, non-financial corporations, financial corporations, or general government). Intra-sector positions may be eliminated in a consolidated sector account, but remain in a non-consolidated presentation.

### 5.3 Whole economy / national balance sheet

A national balance sheet brings together resident institutional sectors and relevant non-financial assets, with the external position represented through claims on and liabilities to non-residents. A country's domestic financial claims and liabilities largely offset when resident sectors are consolidated; net claims on the rest of the world remain as the net financial position vis-à-vis non-residents.

The exact coverage and presentation depend on the national accounts framework and the availability of sectoral balance sheets.

### 5.4 Global consolidated view

At the global boundary, every country is inside the boundary. A claim by a resident of one country on a resident of another is external from each country's perspective, but internal to the world as a whole. Matched cross-border financial claims and liabilities should therefore cancel in a fully reconciled global consolidation, subject to framework-specific exceptions and statistical discrepancies.

This means **global gross financial positions can be very large while the world's aggregate net financial claims on itself are not a measure of net real wealth**. The world cannot, as a whole, be a net creditor to itself.

## 6. Financial claims are not the same as total wealth

Do not use these expressions interchangeably:

- **Gross financial assets:** financial assets held by units within a stated boundary, before subtracting liabilities and before any specified consolidation.
- **Financial liabilities:** obligations or counterpart positions recorded by issuers/debtors.
- **Net financial assets / net financial worth:** financial assets less financial liabilities for the specified unit or boundary.
- **Non-financial assets:** real and other non-financial assets recognised by the framework.
- **Net worth / wealth:** assets less liabilities under a specified definition and valuation; household wealth estimates may have their own coverage and treatment.
- **Market capitalisation:** market value of listed equity; not the same as all corporate assets or total national wealth.
- **Assets under management (AUM):** assets managed under a mandate; not necessarily assets owned beneficially by the manager, nor a separate additional pool to be added to underlying holdings.
- **Capital commitments:** promises or contractual commitments to provide funding; not the same as funded assets, deployed capital, or current holdings.

Financial claims can be important to wealth distribution and control even where they cancel on consolidation. A household's deposit is an asset to that household; the bank's matching liability does not erase the household's access to that deposit. The consolidated view answers a different question from the individual or sectoral view.

## 7. Special cases and cautions

### Equity

Equity is treated as a financial asset of the holder and a liability/equity claim of the issuing corporation in the relevant financial-accounting/statistical framework. It is not a conventional debt obligation requiring repayment of a fixed principal. Its value is residual and depends on the value of the corporation and the valuation method. Do not add market capitalisation to the underlying corporate assets as if both were independent real wealth.

### Monetary gold

Monetary gold is a reserve asset with special treatment in international statistical accounts and does not have an ordinary issuer liability counterpart in the same way as a deposit or bond. Do not force it into the simple matched-claim example. Distinguish monetary gold from non-monetary gold and identify the framework and holder.

### Derivatives

Derivative positions can create financial assets and liabilities whose values change with market prices and risk factors. Notional amounts, gross market values, replacement values, and net exposures are different measures. Do not treat notional amounts as the market value of wealth or sum them with balance-sheet values without a clear purpose and compatible definition. Netting depends on enforceable arrangements and reporting rules.

### Custody, funds, and asset management

An asset manager may manage assets owned by clients or funds. A custodian may hold securities on behalf of beneficial owners. Fund units and the underlying assets can both appear in different institutional balance sheets. Do not count managed or custodial assets as an additional asset pool on top of the owners' claims and the fund's underlying portfolio without analysing the chain and consolidation boundary.

### Pensions and insurance

Pension entitlements and insurance technical reserves represent claims under specific arrangements. Coverage and recognition differ across schemes and statistical frameworks. Do not assume all promised future benefits are captured identically in every national balance sheet.

### Valuation and currency

A stock may be recorded at market value, nominal value, book value, face value, or another prescribed basis. Exchange-rate movements can change the reported domestic-currency value of foreign-currency positions without a new transaction. Record the valuation and currency conversion method before comparing positions.

## 8. A worked example: one bank deposit across three views

Assume a household deposits 100 units in a commercial bank. Ignore fees, capital, reserves, and all other transactions for this illustration.

| View | Household | Bank | Combined interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Household balance sheet | Deposit asset +100 | — | Household owns a claim on bank |
| Bank balance sheet | — | Deposit liability +100 | Bank owes the deposit |
| Combined household + bank | Financial asset +100 | Financial liability +100 | Matched claim and liability cancel on consolidation |

**What this means in ordinary language:** The household has 100 units available as a bank deposit, and the bank has an obligation to honour that deposit under the account terms and applicable rules. Looking at the household alone, the deposit is an asset. Looking at the bank alone, it is a liability. Looking at both together, the deposit does not create 100 units of additional net wealth for the combined pair. It remains important because it determines the household's claim and the bank's funding obligation.

## 9. Measurement implications for the global exploration

A credible global financial balance-sheet investigation needs several complementary views rather than one undifferentiated total.

| Analytical view | What it answers | Key data needs |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument / claim | What kinds of financial claims exist? | Deposits, loans, debt securities, equity, funds, derivatives, insurance/pensions, other accounts |
| Holder sector | Who holds the assets? | Households, non-financial corporations, financial subsectors, government, rest of world |
| Issuer / debtor sector | Who issues or owes? | Counterpart sector and instrument |
| Residence | Which economy's resident unit holds or owes the position? | Residence-based sector accounts and external counterpart detail |
| Ownership / control | Who ultimately benefits or controls? | Beneficial ownership, fund and custody chains; often incomplete |
| Consolidation | Which intra-boundary positions are eliminated? | Consistent sector and national balance-sheet tables |
| Valuation | At what value are positions recorded? | Market/nominal/book/face values, exchange-rate basis |
| Time | At what date does the stock apply? | Reference date, frequency, revisions and vintage |

The global exercise should be built bottom-up:
1. Define the institutional and geographic boundary.
2. Select an accounting framework and reference period.
3. Identify resident sectors and instrument classifications.
4. Obtain financial assets and liabilities by sector and counterpart where possible.
5. Identify consolidation treatment and remove only positions that are matched within the stated boundary.
6. Reconcile domestic and external positions, including cross-border claims.
7. Investigate statistical discrepancies, missing sectors, unrecorded positions, and valuation differences.
8. Report gross positions, net positions, and non-financial assets separately.
9. State coverage and uncertainty; do not label a partial estimate as the global total.

## 10. What this foundation establishes — and what it does not

### Established conceptually
- A balance sheet is a stock statement at a date.
- Net worth is assets less liabilities under a defined accounting boundary.
- Most financial assets are counterpart claims on another unit.
- Consolidation eliminates matched positions within a stated boundary.
- National and global consolidation change which claims are internal and which are external.
- Gross financial positions remain analytically important even where they cancel in consolidated net terms.
- Financial assets, financial liabilities, net financial worth, non-financial assets, and total wealth are distinct.
- AUM, commitments, deployed capital, market capitalisation, and underlying assets must not be indiscriminately added together.

### Not established empirically
- The total value of global financial assets or liabilities.
- Complete global sectoral coverage.
- A reconciled global balance sheet by instrument, sector, and country.
- The size of unrecorded, offshore, or otherwise missing positions.
- A comparable global distribution of beneficial ownership and effective control.
- Whether any candidate source set is sufficient to produce a robust global estimate.

## 11. Statistical framework: the System of National Accounts

The most coherent starting architecture for national sectoral balance sheets is the **System of National Accounts (SNA)**, rather than attempting to assemble a global balance sheet from unrelated financial datasets.

The United Nations Statistical Commission adopted the **2025 SNA** at its 56th session in 2025. It updates the 2008 SNA while retaining its basic theoretical framework and adding or clarifying treatment for developments including globalization, digitalization, emerging financial issues, Islamic finance, and the informal economy. The 2025 SNA is the current international statistical standard; however, adoption of a standard does not mean every country has already implemented it in its published data. The implementation strategy and the vintage/standard actually used by each dataset must be checked.

Official framework:
- UN Statistics Division, [System of National Accounts 2025](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/econstats/national-accounts/sna2025).
- [2025 SNA searchable pre-edit text](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/nationalaccount/docs/2025_SNA_Pre-edit.pdf).

The SNA's sequence of accounts links:
- Current and capital accounts.
- Financial transactions during a period.
- Other changes in assets and liabilities, including revaluations and other volume changes.
- Opening and closing balance sheets for institutional sectors and the rest of the world.

This architecture matters because the closing stock is not explained by transactions alone. The framework provides the accounting bridge needed to distinguish new lending or securities purchases from price changes, exchange-rate movements, write-offs, reclassifications, and other changes.

### 11.1 Complementary frameworks and datasets

- **IMF Monetary and Financial Statistics (MFS):** important for financial-corporation stocks and flows, but it does not replace the full SNA sectoral sequence across all institutional sectors.
- **IMF Financial Soundness Indicators (FSI):** macroprudential indicators and selected underlying balance-sheet/income-statement series for financial and non-financial sectors. The IMF's November 2026 data brief reports FSI sectoral data for 156 countries, with 60 reporting some data through 2026 Q2; coverage varies by country, sector, indicator, and frequency. This is valuable evidence, but not a complete globally harmonized balance-sheet census.
- **OECD Financial Accounts and Balance Sheets:** provides sector- and instrument-based national financial accounts and stock balance sheets for participating economies. The OECD describes annual and quarterly balance-sheet stocks and financial-account flows, including counterpart information; coverage and consolidation must be checked for each country and table.
- **Eurostat / ECB sector accounts:** offer a comparatively detailed regional example of the integrated sequence, including balance sheets, financial transactions, revaluations, and other changes in volume. They are a methodological benchmark and regional dataset, not a substitute for global coverage.

Official sources:
- IMF [Financial Soundness Indicators](https://data.imf.org/Datasets/FSIC).
- IMF [FSI Balance Sheet, Income Statement and Memorandum Series](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/IMF.STA%3AFSIBSIS).
- IMF [November 2026 FSI data brief](https://data.imf.org/en/news/imf%20data%20brief%20november%2011).
- OECD [Financial Accounts and Balance Sheets](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/financial-accounts-and-balance-sheets.html).
- OECD [Annual Financial Accounts, consolidated](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/vis?ac=false&bp=true&df%5Bag%5D=OECD.SDD.NAD&df%5Bds%5D=dsDisseminateFinalDMZ&df%5Bid%5D=DSD_NASEC20%40DF_T610R_A&df%5Bvs%5D=1.1&fc=Topic&fs%5B0%5D=Topic%2C0%7CEconomy%23ECO%23&pg=0&snb=307).
- Eurostat [Institutional sector accounts methodology](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/sector-accounts/methodology).
- Eurostat [Financial flows and stocks metadata](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/nasq_10_f_esms.htm).

### 11.2 Framework decision for this exploration

Use the 2025 SNA as the **conceptual target architecture** for the national sectoral-balance-sheet investigation. For actual observations, record the standard and methodology used by each country/source (which may still be 2008 SNA, ESA 2010, or another documented basis during transition). Do not silently relabel older observations as 2025 SNA data or assume that datasets are comparable merely because they share an SNA lineage.

Use IMF MFS and FSI, OECD financial accounts, Eurostat/ECB, national financial accounts, and IMF external accounts as complementary evidence sources. Each has a distinct scope. Reconcile rather than substitute.

3. How are national balance sheets, financial accounts, and international investment positions connected?
4. What is the difference between non-consolidated and consolidated sector accounts in the actual available datasets?
5. Can we construct a small, reproducible country pilot and reconcile its domestic and external positions?
6. What are the largest coverage gaps: households, non-financial corporations, non-bank financial institutions, government, offshore entities, or instrument classes?

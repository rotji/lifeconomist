# Air, Atmosphere, and Climate Systems

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. This is a broad navigation map, not a technical assessment, market validation, or selection of opportunities. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey has covered the major environmental territories.

## Scope

This territory covers atmospheric conditions, air pollution, emissions, greenhouse gases, climate information, climate-related risks, and technologies and services used to observe, prevent, reduce, capture, manage, or respond to atmospheric and climate impacts.

## Principal branches

1. **Outdoor air quality:** particulate matter, ozone, nitrogen oxides, sulphur dioxide, and other ambient pollutants.
2. **Indoor air quality:** ventilation, indoor pollutants, mould, filtration, and building air management.
3. **Industrial emissions:** monitoring and control of pollutants from factories, power plants, and industrial processes.
4. **Atmospheric monitoring and measurement:** weather stations, air sensors, atmospheric sampling, and observation networks.
5. **Greenhouse-gas emissions:** measurement, reporting, accounting, inventories, and emissions-reduction services.
6. **Carbon capture and removal:** point-source capture, direct air capture, biological removal, and mineralisation.
7. **Atmospheric treatment and pollution control:** filtration, scrubbers, dust suppression, ventilation, and emissions-control equipment.
8. **Weather and climate information:** forecasting, climate data, modelling, analytics, and decision support.
9. **Climate adaptation and resilience:** heat management, resilient infrastructure, agricultural adaptation, and risk reduction.
10. **Climate-risk intelligence:** physical and transition risk, exposure mapping, scenario analysis, and climate-related financial information.
11. **Carbon markets and environmental claims:** project development, credits, measurement, reporting, verification, registries, and claims integrity.
12. **Atmospheric research and technology:** atmospheric chemistry, climate modelling, observation systems, and emerging interventions.

## Cross-cutting opportunity positions

Potential positions include sensor and equipment supply; installation and maintenance; monitoring networks; sampling and laboratory coordination; emissions inventories; data processing and analytics; forecasting and risk products; compliance support; mitigation and adaptation project services; measurement and verification; and carbon-market integrity services. These are discovery areas, not validated business opportunities.

## Questions to preserve for later vertical exploration

- Which pollutants or climate variables matter to which customers, regulators, and communities?
- What measurement accuracy, spatial coverage, update frequency, and verification standards are required?
- Which interventions genuinely reduce exposure or emissions, and what trade-offs or residual impacts arise?
- How are climate risks translated into operational, infrastructure, agricultural, insurance, and financial decisions?
- What evidence supports carbon-removal permanence, additionality, leakage control, and environmental integrity?
- What can be delivered through existing monitoring providers, laboratories, technical specialists, or public datasets?

## Boundaries and overlaps

This territory overlaps with energy (emissions and transition), buildings (ventilation and cooling), transport and industry, water and agriculture (climate impacts), environmental intelligence (measurement and remote sensing), environmental risk (hazards and resilience), and environmental economics (carbon markets and regulation).

Carbon is an important subject within this territory, but it does not define the entire environmental economy.

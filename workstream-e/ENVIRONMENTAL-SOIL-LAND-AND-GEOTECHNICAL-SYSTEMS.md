# Soil, Land, and Geotechnical Systems

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. This is a broad navigation map, not a technical assessment, market validation, or selection of opportunities. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey has covered the major environmental territories.

## Scope

This territory covers soils as living and physical systems, land condition and productivity, soil-water interactions, earth materials, subsurface conditions, terrain stability, and the technical services and infrastructure used to assess, manage, protect, engineer, and restore land.

The earlier **Land and Terrestrial Resources** exploration focused on land as a spatial and physical resource, land cover and use, access and rights, site suitability, observation, stewardship, and allocation. This territory is related but distinct: it focuses on soil properties and processes, land degradation and condition, and geotechnical and earth-system behaviour. Avoid repeating the earlier land-resource map; use cross-references where land access, site assessment, or mapping is relevant.

## Principal branches

1. **Soil properties and classification:** soil types, texture, structure, depth, composition, physical and chemical characteristics, and soil classification.
2. **Soil health and fertility:** organic matter, nutrients, biological activity, soil function, fertility assessment, and soil-health services.
3. **Soil-water systems:** infiltration, permeability, water retention, drainage, moisture monitoring, salinity, and waterlogging.
4. **Soil degradation and erosion:** erosion by water or wind, compaction, crusting, acidification, salinisation, desertification, and land degradation.
5. **Soil contamination and pollution:** contaminated soils, pollutants, testing, exposure pathways, containment, and links to remediation.
6. **Soil conservation and management:** conservation practices, erosion control, soil cover, sustainable land management, and monitoring.
7. **Soil amendments and improvement inputs:** compost, biochar, lime, fertilisers, conditioners, and other amendments, assessed for suitability, quality, safety, and actual performance.
8. **Terrain, geomorphology, and earth processes:** slopes, landforms, weathering, sediment movement, ground instability, and geomorphological assessment.
9. **Geotechnical investigation and testing:** site investigation, boreholes, sampling, laboratory tests, in-situ testing, and ground characterisation.
10. **Ground engineering and foundations:** foundations, retaining structures, ground improvement, excavation support, and construction-ground interfaces.
11. **Slope stability, landslides, and subsidence:** hazard assessment, monitoring, stabilisation, early warning, and risk reduction.
12. **Earth materials and borrow resources:** aggregates, sand, gravel, clay, laterite, and other earth materials, including sourcing, quality, impacts, and restoration obligations.
13. **Land drainage and earthworks:** grading, drainage, embankments, cut-and-fill, erosion control, and site preparation.
14. **Soil and ground intelligence:** soil mapping, geospatial datasets, field observation, remote sensing, change detection, and decision-support information.

## Cross-cutting opportunity positions

Potential positions include soil sampling and testing coordination; field data collection; soil and terrain mapping; laboratory services; geotechnical investigation support; monitoring and early-warning systems; supply and distribution of verified soil inputs; erosion-control installation and maintenance; site assessment; and coordination with qualified engineers, laboratories, agronomists, and remediation operators. These are discovery areas, not validated businesses or permission to perform regulated professional work.

## Questions to preserve for later vertical exploration

- Which soil or ground properties matter for a specific user decision, and what measurement method is reliable enough?
- How do local geology, climate, land use, vegetation, and management affect soil condition?
- Which soil amendments or conservation interventions have evidence of effectiveness under the relevant conditions?
- What are the risks of contaminated material, unsafe inputs, erosion, slope failure, or poorly designed earthworks?
- Which tasks require licensed or qualified professionals, permits, laboratory capability, or formal liability cover?
- Who pays for testing, monitoring, ground investigation, maintenance, or risk reduction, and what alternatives do they use?
- Can existing laboratories, surveyors, engineers, equipment owners, and field operators provide the technical capability while an entrant performs a bounded coordination, data, or support role?

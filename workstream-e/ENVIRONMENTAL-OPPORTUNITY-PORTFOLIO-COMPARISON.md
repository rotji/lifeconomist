## 1. Purpose and scope

The first candidate longlist contains 15 cross-territory investigation areas. This document compares their dependencies and available evidence to propose a manageable first research sequence.

The comparison is not a numeric ranking. The evidence is not sufficiently comparable, and several candidates have not been examined. The sequence prioritises candidates where:
- a real-world environmental or operational issue is visible in credible sources;
- some initial evidence or data appears accessible;
- the problem can be investigated before buying equipment or building a product;
- the investigation can clarify a user, payer, workflow, and existing provider landscape;
- findings may reveal several possible participation positions, not just a single business model.

## 2. Evidence observed in the first public-source scan

### 2.1 Environmental and geospatial information

- The Federal Ministry of Environment describes a Survey and Mapping Department whose responsibilities include spatial data collection, land-use/land-cover mapping, watershed mapping, environmental monitoring, and land information systems. This demonstrates an institutional geospatial function; it does **not** establish that its datasets are openly downloadable or that a private service has paying customers.
- The National Centre for Remote Sensing in Jos lists satellite data services, natural-resource mapping, forestry and environment, water resources, disaster management, ecological and climate studies among its areas of work. This indicates an existing national technical ecosystem and possible institutional capability to understand, learn from, or partner with; access, terms, and commercial arrangements remain unknown.
- FAO published a 2024 land-cover classification for four Nigerian states in January 2026, derived from Sentinel-2 imagery at 30-metre resolution and reporting 83% overall accuracy. The geographic coverage is limited to selected states, and 83% overall accuracy does not guarantee suitability for every local decision or class.
- UNESCO's Nigeria Flood and Drought Monitor dataset description, published in January 2026, describes a national interface combining ground observations, satellite data, and models, with maps, time series, statistics, and warning information. This is an existing public-interest monitoring capability. Any additional service must identify an unmet decision or user need rather than simply reproduce an existing map.
- A 2024 systematic review notes the absence of a current official national land-cover dataset at the time of its review and stresses the need to validate global land-cover products for spatial/temporal resolution, bias, and application suitability. This should be reconciled with newer datasets and agency updates before making claims about current national coverage.

**Implication:** Geospatial and environmental intelligence has a credible evidence base and visible institutions/data sources to investigate. The key unresolved question is not whether maps can be made; it is whether a defined user has a decision that existing sources do not adequately support, and whether the user would pay for a more reliable, timely, local, or actionable result.

### 2.2 Agricultural and post-harvest losses

- The National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services' Agricultural Performance Survey of the 2022 wet season reports crop post-harvest losses across North-Central states. It records estimated losses for selected crops and reports a 60% estimated loss for cowpea in the Federal Capital Territory for that survey year. The report also identifies loss stages and causes such as poor drying, storage, pests/disease, flood, insecurity, and transport.
- A 2025 policy/research publication on clean innovative technologies in Nigeria's agricultural sector discusses post-harvest losses and cites estimates for perishable foods and selected crop groups. These headline estimates are not interchangeable with a specific commodity, location, season, or measurement method.
- The NAERLS result is a historical, survey-specific signal—not a current FCT loss rate, not a guarantee of a paying customer, and not evidence that cold storage is the correct intervention. The commodity, stage, cause, and actor must be investigated separately.

**Implication:** Agricultural loss visibility and post-harvest coordination merit an early vertical investigation because a geographically specific official survey provides a starting point. The next task is to identify the commodity and stage where the loss occurs, the actor who bears it, existing practices, and whether information, handling, storage, logistics, processing, or buyer coordination is the binding constraint.

### 2.3 Waste systems and producer responsibility

- Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Environment publishes a National Policy on Solid Waste Management.
- NESREA publishes EPR registration guidance and forms for producers, producer-responsibility organisations, recyclers, and collectors, and describes permit and registration processes.
- The International Telecommunication Union's Nigeria e-waste country page describes the country's EPR framework while noting continuing challenges around formal collection, recycling, traceability, financing, and integration of informal actors.
- A 2026 policy brief on inclusive waste governance argues that informal waste workers are important to collection and recycling and calls for formal recognition, protection, and partnership. This is a policy argument and should be treated as a source perspective, not as proof that specific contracts or funded programmes are available in a given location.

**Implication:** Waste-stream coordination and compliance intelligence have a credible institutional and regulatory context. However, local material volumes, existing collectors, buyers, recycler capacity, contract terms, payment flows, and the user's ability to enter the system remain unverified. This candidate should be investigated through a specific material stream and locality, not as a generic recycling business.

## 4. Proposed initial investigation sequence

This is a **research sequence**, not a final direction for Lifeconomist and not a commitment to build a product.

### Investigation 1 — ENV-C05: One bounded geospatial/environmental intelligence use case

Why first:
- Initial public-source evidence and institutions are identifiable.
- The investigation can begin with desk research and data inspection rather than asset ownership.
- It can reveal whether the value lies in data discovery, validation, local change detection, site/asset monitoring, reporting, or integration with an existing operator.
- It provides a useful test of the project's geospatial and evidence-quality capabilities.

Boundary: do not build a general environmental dashboard. Choose one asset class or decision and one defined geography only after identifying a plausible user. Potential examples for later selection include land-cover change for a defined project, flood exposure of a defined asset group, or monitoring of a specific environmental asset. These are examples, not selected use cases.

First evidence tasks:
1. Catalogue the exact datasets and platforms, coverage, dates, resolution, licences, and access conditions.
2. Compare overlapping sources and record contradictions and limitations.
3. Identify one decision-maker group and the decision they need to make.
4. Ask what current source or workflow they use and what remains unsolved.
5. Create a small reproducible sample analysis only if the data are fit for the question.
6. Test whether the result changes a real decision or saves a measurable cost.

### Investigation 2 — ENV-C07/C08: One commodity and one post-harvest stage

Why second:
- A Nigerian official survey provides a historical, geographically specific starting signal.
- It is possible to investigate actual practices and actors before recommending equipment or capital.
- The investigation may reveal opportunities in handling, drying, storage, transport, buyer coordination, records, or quality rather than assuming cold storage.

Boundary: do not generalise the historical FCT cowpea estimate to current conditions or to other commodities. First establish whether the same issue persists, for which commodity, in which locality, at what stage, and who pays for a solution.

First evidence tasks:
1. Read the NAERLS survey tables and methods for the specific North-Central/FCT observations.
2. Identify the commodity, survey period, loss range, reported causes, and location.
3. Map the chain from harvest to buyer for that commodity.
4. Identify who owns the produce at each stage and who bears the loss.
5. Record existing storage, drying, transport, aggregation, and market services.
6. Compare low-cost operational/coordination changes with equipment-intensive interventions.
7. Establish current-season evidence before estimating market size or savings.

### Investigation 3 — ENV-C01/C02: One waste material stream and its existing chain

Why third:
- Policy, EPR, and formal/informal system sources provide an institutional entry point.
- The investigation can map existing actors and transactions before proposing collection assets or processing machinery.
- It may uncover roles in reliable sourcing, quality control, traceability, compliance records, logistics, or buyer coordination.

Boundary: do not begin with a generic recycling plant or machine. Choose one material stream and one locality; distinguish waste generators, collectors, aggregators, processors, buyers, regulators, and informal workers.

First evidence tasks:
1. Select a material stream only after establishing local visibility and access.
2. Map where it is generated, how it is currently collected, and where it goes.
3. Record quality, contamination, volume, seasonality, price, and rejected material.
4. Identify current collectors, aggregators, recyclers, buyers, and formal/informal relationships.
5. Verify permits, EPR rules, health and safety, and local government requirements.
6. Identify who pays for collection, sorting, transport, or recovered material.
7. Test whether a specific coordination or information gap is real.

## 5. Why other candidates are not discarded

The remaining candidates are held, not rejected. Some may become higher priority when a real customer, project, asset, dataset, incident, contract, or partner becomes visible. For example:
- Water testing may advance if a recurring user need and accredited laboratory route are identified.
- Risk alerts may advance if a defined asset owner has an actionable information gap beyond existing public warnings.
- Nature monitoring may advance if a funded project and measurable outcome exist.
- Biological conversion may advance when a reliable feedstock and buyer are established.
- Compliance support may merge with a specific waste, water, infrastructure, or industrial workflow.

## 6. Decision requested from the user

The assistant proposes the above three investigations as the first portfolio sequence because each has a distinct evidence route and can be investigated before committing to major capital. The user retains the decision to accept, reorder, pause, or replace the sequence.

No candidate is a final Lifeconomist direction. The final direction should be chosen only after the user has reviewed the accumulated evidence, practical access, constraints, and potential roles.

### Important limitations
- The sources demonstrate public datasets, institutional functions, policies, and reported problem signals; they do not establish a paying customer, a commercial market, current local operating conditions, or a viable business.
- The FAO land-cover dataset covers four selected states, not all Nigeria.
- The NAERLS loss observations concern the 2022 wet season and must not be represented as current rates.
- Public data availability does not imply unrestricted commercial reuse; inspect dataset licences and access conditions.
- Institutional programmes and policies do not prove procurement budgets, open tenders, or partnership availability.
- All local fieldwork requires lawful access, consent where appropriate, safety, and respect for community and property rights.

# Energy and Environmental Technologies

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. This is a broad navigation map, not a technical assessment, market validation, or selection of opportunities. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey has covered the major environmental territories.

## Scope

This territory covers the systems, technologies, equipment, infrastructure, services, markets, and intelligence involved in producing, storing, converting, distributing, and using energy, alongside technologies that reduce environmental burdens or improve resource performance.

## Principal branches

1. **Renewable electricity generation:** solar, wind, hydropower, geothermal, and marine energy.
2. **Distributed energy systems:** mini-grids, microgrids, rooftop solar, decentralised generation, and community or facility-scale systems.
3. **Energy storage:** batteries, pumped hydro, thermal storage, hydrogen storage, and other storage approaches.
4. **Bioenergy and biomass systems:** biogas, biofuels, biomass heat and electricity, and energy from suitable waste-derived feedstocks.
5. **Hydrogen and alternative energy carriers:** hydrogen, ammonia, and other carriers, including production, storage, transport, and use.
6. **Grid infrastructure and power systems:** transmission, distribution, substations, transformers, inverters, grid management, balancing, and reliability.
7. **Energy efficiency and demand management:** efficient appliances, motors, buildings, industrial systems, audits, controls, and load management.
8. **Heating, cooling, and thermal systems:** heat pumps, refrigeration, industrial heat, district cooling, thermal storage, and waste-heat recovery.
9. **Clean cooking and household energy:** improved cookstoves, electric cooking, cleaner fuels, and household energy distribution and services.
10. **Industrial energy systems:** process heat, electrification, cogeneration, energy recovery, and industrial energy optimisation.
11. **Energy equipment and manufacturing:** panels, turbines, batteries, inverters, controls, components, spare parts, and associated supply chains.
12. **Energy services and project operations:** installation, maintenance, leasing, project development, operation, financing, and energy-as-a-service.
13. **Energy measurement and intelligence:** smart meters, energy audits, forecasting, monitoring, optimisation, performance measurement, and verification.
14. **Energy markets and commercial mechanisms:** tariffs, procurement, power trading, wheeling, power-purchase agreements, and related market arrangements.

## Cross-cutting opportunity positions

Potential value-chain positions include resource and site assessment; equipment supply and distribution; project design and development; installation and commissioning; operations and maintenance; aggregation and demand management; metering and analytics; financing and insurance coordination; and customer support. These are areas to investigate, not claims that each is viable or accessible.

## Questions to preserve for later vertical exploration

- Which technologies are technically mature, emerging, or still highly dependent on demonstration?
- What are the real input, storage, conversion, transmission, and end-use constraints?
- Which costs, tariffs, regulations, reliability problems, and customer needs shape each market?
- What roles can be performed through existing providers or infrastructure, and what permissions, skills, capital, and liability do they require?
- What are the lifecycle impacts, including land, water, materials, emissions, waste, and end-of-life management?

## Boundaries and overlaps

This territory overlaps with materials and minerals (batteries, conductors, catalysts), water (hydropower, cooling, treatment energy), waste and biomass (energy recovery), buildings and infrastructure, climate and emissions, and environmental measurement. Record cross-links rather than forcing each opportunity into only one category.

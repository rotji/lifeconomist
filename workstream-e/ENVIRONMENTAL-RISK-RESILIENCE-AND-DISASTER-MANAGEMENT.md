# Environmental Risk, Resilience, and Disaster Management

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. Broad navigation map only; not a hazard assessment, emergency plan, insurance opinion, or market validation. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey is complete.

## Scope

This territory covers the identification, assessment, prevention, reduction, preparation for, response to, and recovery from hazards and disruptions that affect people, ecosystems, infrastructure, businesses, and environmental systems. It includes natural hazards, climate-related risks, technological and industrial incidents, compound risks, early warning, resilience planning, emergency operations, and recovery.

Risk is not the same as hazard. A hazard is a potentially damaging event or process; risk depends on exposure, vulnerability, capacity, and the likelihood and severity of consequences. Resilience concerns the ability to anticipate, absorb, adapt to, recover from, and where possible transform systems after disruption. A risk map alone does not reduce risk unless it informs decisions and action.

## Principal branches

1. **Flood and inundation risk:** river floods, flash floods, urban flooding, coastal flooding, drainage failure, flood exposure, forecasting, protection, and recovery.
2. **Drought and water-security risk:** meteorological, agricultural, and hydrological drought; water scarcity, competing demands, drought monitoring, contingency planning, and recovery.
3. **Extreme heat and temperature stress:** heatwaves, urban heat, occupational exposure, crop and livestock stress, cooling demand, heat-health planning, and adaptation.
4. **Storms, severe weather, and wind hazards:** intense rainfall, thunderstorms, damaging winds, lightning, tropical systems where relevant, forecasting, preparedness, and asset protection.
5. **Erosion, landslides, subsidence, and ground hazards:** slope failure, coastal erosion, ground movement, sinkholes, unstable terrain, monitoring, land-use planning, and protective measures.
6. **Wildfire and vegetation-fire risk:** fire weather, fuel conditions, ignition, landscape management, detection, response, smoke exposure, and post-fire recovery.
7. **Coastal, marine, and shoreline risks:** storm surge, sea-level rise, coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion, marine pollution incidents, and coastal ecosystem resilience.
8. **Climate risk and adaptation planning:** physical climate risks, transition-related dependencies where relevant, scenario analysis, adaptation options, and resilience investment.
9. **Ecosystem and biodiversity risk:** ecosystem degradation, habitat fragmentation, invasive species, species decline, ecological tipping concerns, and nature-dependent service disruption.
10. **Agricultural and food-system risk:** crop failure, livestock disease, pest outbreaks, input disruptions, post-harvest losses, supply shocks, and food-security impacts.
11. **Industrial and technological environmental incidents:** chemical releases, pipeline failures, mine incidents, tailings failures, industrial fires, explosions, and accidental pollution.
12. **Infrastructure and utility disruption:** power, water, transport, telecommunications, drainage, and critical facility failures, including cascading and interdependent effects.
13. **Public and occupational environmental exposure:** air pollution episodes, contaminated water, heat stress, hazardous substances, sanitation failures, and exposure prevention.
14. **Early warning, forecasting, and alert systems:** observation networks, thresholds, risk communication, last-mile alerts, community warning systems, and response triggers.
15. **Preparedness, continuity, and emergency operations:** contingency plans, drills, response logistics, emergency supplies, incident command, mutual aid, and business continuity.
16. **Disaster recovery and reconstruction:** damage and needs assessment, debris management, restoration, resilient rebuilding, livelihood recovery, and lessons learned.
17. **Risk finance, insurance, and transfer mechanisms:** insurance, reinsurance, parametric products, catastrophe finance, contingency funds, risk pooling, and public-private mechanisms.
18. **Community resilience and local adaptation:** local risk knowledge, inclusive planning, resilient livelihoods, community infrastructure, evacuation access, and participation of exposed groups.
19. **Compound, cascading, and systemic risk:** interacting hazards, infrastructure dependencies, supply-chain disruption, correlated failures, and cross-sector propagation.
20. **Risk governance, standards, and accountability:** risk ownership, land-use rules, building standards, emergency authority, liability, public communication, and post-event accountability.

## Questions for later vertical exploration

- What is the hazard, what evidence supports its likelihood and severity, and what is the relevant time horizon?
- Who and what is exposed, how vulnerable are they, and what capacities already exist?
- Are multiple hazards or infrastructure dependencies likely to interact or cascade?
- What observations, forecasts, thresholds, and warning lead times are available, and how reliable are they?
- Who receives the warning, understands it, trusts it, and can act on it?
- Which interventions reduce risk rather than merely transfer it to another place, group, or time?
- Who owns the risk and has authority and budget to implement mitigation?
- What standards, emergency powers, insurance terms, disclosure duties, and liability rules apply?
- How will resilience or avoided losses be measured, and what counterfactual is appropriate?
- Can local communities, public agencies, utilities, operators, insurers, researchers, and response organisations provide existing capabilities?

# Materials, Minerals, Chemicals, and Advanced Materials

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. This is a broad navigation map, not a technical assessment, market validation, or selection of opportunities. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey has covered the major environmental territories.

## Scope

This territory covers the natural and processed materials that underpin products, infrastructure, energy systems, agriculture, manufacturing, environmental technologies, and the wider economy. It includes mineral resources, extraction and processing, industrial chemicals, conventional and advanced materials, material recovery and substitution, and the services and information systems that support their development and use.

## Principal branches

1. **Mineral resources and geological materials:** metallic ores, industrial minerals, construction minerals, clays, silica, limestone, phosphate, graphite, and other geological resources.
2. **Mining and mineral extraction:** exploration, resource estimation, mine development, extraction methods, mine operations, and closure planning.
3. **Mineral processing and beneficiation:** crushing, grinding, screening, sorting, concentration, separation, dewatering, and upgrading of mineral feedstocks.
4. **Metals and metallurgy:** iron and steel, aluminium, copper, critical and specialty metals, smelting, refining, alloying, casting, and metalworking.
5. **Industrial minerals and construction materials:** cement, lime, aggregates, sand, gravel, gypsum, ceramics, glass, and related products.
6. **Polymers, plastics, and elastomeric materials:** conventional and engineered polymers, rubber, additives, compounding, recycling, and alternative material systems.
7. **Chemicals and chemical processing:** basic chemicals, industrial intermediates, solvents, acids, alkalis, fertiliser chemicals, specialty chemicals, and process engineering.
8. **Advanced and functional materials:** composites, nanomaterials, membranes, catalysts, coatings, adsorbents, electronic materials, and other engineered materials.
9. **Battery and energy materials:** electrode materials, electrolytes, separators, conductive materials, catalysts, hydrogen-related materials, and recycling of energy-system materials.
10. **Material recovery, recycling, and circularity:** recovery of metals, polymers, glass, minerals, catalysts, and other materials from industrial residues, products, and end-of-life streams.
11. **Material substitution and resource efficiency:** reducing material intensity, replacing scarce or hazardous inputs, extending service life, improving durability, and designing for repair and recovery.
12. **Materials testing and characterisation:** chemical analysis, microscopy, mechanical testing, thermal analysis, surface analysis, quality control, and standards testing.
13. **Materials research, modelling, and informatics:** computational materials science, simulation, databases, machine learning, materials discovery, and research workflow infrastructure.
14. **Materials manufacturing and product conversion:** formulation, mixing, forming, sintering, coating, extrusion, additive manufacturing, fabrication, and component production.
15. **Materials supply chains and market intelligence:** sourcing, trading, procurement, specifications, traceability, logistics, price intelligence, and supplier qualification.
16. **Mine, process, and materials environmental management:** tailings, waste rock, process residues, water use, emissions, hazardous substances, site rehabilitation, and lifecycle assessment.

## Cross-cutting opportunity positions

Potential positions include geological and supplier intelligence; sampling and testing coordination; equipment and reagent supply; processing and toll-treatment services; material recovery and aggregation; quality assurance; distribution and procurement support; laboratory and modelling services; technical data products; traceability; maintenance; and environmental monitoring. These are areas to investigate, not validated opportunities or substitutes for qualified technical work.

## Questions to preserve for later vertical exploration

- Which resources and materials are available, where are they located, and what evidence supports their quantity and quality?
- What processing steps, energy, water, reagents, equipment, and technical skills are required?
- Which outputs meet actual customer specifications, and what happens to impurities, by-products, tailings, and other residues?
- How do commodity prices, import dependence, logistics, power reliability, scale, and customer qualification affect economics?
- Which materials are strategically important to energy, construction, manufacturing, agriculture, water, and environmental technologies?
- Where can modelling, data, testing, certification, sourcing, or coordination create value without owning extraction or manufacturing assets?
- What health, safety, environmental, community, land-rights, and closure obligations apply?

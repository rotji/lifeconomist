# Biodiversity, Ecosystems, and Biological Resources

**Status:** Initial horizontal territory map documented 2026-11-06. This is a broad navigation map, not an ecological assessment, market validation, or selection of opportunities. Vertical exploration is deferred until the horizontal survey has covered the major environmental territories.

## Scope

This territory covers the diversity of living organisms, the ecosystems and habitats in which they exist, the ecological processes and services they support, and the biological resources and knowledge systems connected to them. It includes conservation and sustainable use, ecosystem monitoring, restoration, biological materials, and nature-related services and markets.

Biodiversity is broader than wildlife or forests alone. It includes diversity within species, between species, and across ecosystems, as well as ecological interactions and functions. Economic exploration must not treat living systems merely as extractable stock: rights, ecological limits, community relationships, and long-term ecosystem integrity matter.

## Principal branches

1. **Forests and woodland ecosystems:** natural forests, plantations, woodland, forest structure, forest condition, sustainable management, and forest monitoring.
2. **Wetlands and freshwater ecosystems:** marshes, swamps, floodplains, lakes, rivers, streams, riparian habitats, and their ecological functions.
3. **Grasslands, savannas, and dryland ecosystems:** rangelands, savannas, shrublands, dry forests, desert ecosystems, and their management and restoration.
4. **Marine and coastal ecosystems:** mangroves, estuaries, seagrass beds, coral reefs, coastal wetlands, open-ocean habitats, and shoreline ecosystems.
5. **Species and wildlife systems:** plants, animals, fungi, microorganisms, species distributions, population dynamics, threatened species, and wildlife management.
6. **Genetic and biological diversity:** genetic resources, varieties, breeds, microbial diversity, biological collections, and the governance of access and benefit-sharing.
7. **Ecosystem functions and services:** pollination, nutrient cycling, water regulation, soil formation, carbon storage, coastal protection, habitat provision, and other ecosystem functions.
8. **Conservation and protected-area management:** protected areas, conservation planning, habitat connectivity, anti-poaching systems, stewardship, and conservation operations.
9. **Ecological restoration and rehabilitation:** habitat restoration, reforestation, wetland rehabilitation, landscape recovery, invasive-site recovery, and post-disturbance restoration.
10. **Invasive species, pests, and ecological threats:** invasive organisms, wildlife disease, pests, habitat fragmentation, overexploitation, and ecological threat management.
11. **Biological resources and non-timber products:** medicinal and aromatic plants, fibres, resins, gums, natural dyes, wild foods, fungi, algae, and other biological materials, subject to sustainable-use and access rules.
12. **Algae and aquatic biological resources:** microalgae, macroalgae, seaweed, aquatic biomass, cultivation systems, harvesting, processing, and downstream applications.
13. **Ecological and biodiversity assessment:** field surveys, species identification, habitat assessment, ecological baselines, impact assessment, and biodiversity monitoring.
14. **Nature data and biodiversity intelligence:** species observations, habitat maps, acoustic monitoring, camera traps, environmental DNA, remote sensing, databases, and decision-support systems.
15. **Nature-based solutions:** ecosystem-based approaches to water management, coastal protection, urban cooling, erosion control, and climate adaptation, with outcomes verified in context.
16. **Nature finance and biodiversity-related markets:** conservation finance, ecosystem-service payments, biodiversity credits and claims, nature-related disclosure, and project verification, with careful scrutiny of integrity and benefit distribution.

## Cross-cutting opportunity positions

Potential positions include ecological field-survey support; species and habitat data collection; mapping and monitoring; equipment and data services; restoration project coordination; nursery and native-plant supply; sustainable biological-resource sourcing; traceability; laboratory and identification services; conservation operations support; and environmental reporting. These are areas to investigate, not validated business opportunities or permission to undertake specialist ecological work without appropriate competence and approvals.

## Questions to preserve for later vertical exploration

- What ecological asset, species, habitat, process, or service is involved, and what evidence establishes its condition and significance?
- Who has legal rights, customary rights, stewardship responsibilities, or decision-making authority over the resource or territory?
- What ecological limits, seasonality, regeneration rates, and cumulative impacts constrain use?
- What methods can reliably measure biodiversity or ecosystem outcomes, and what uncertainty remains?
- Who benefits from ecosystem services, who bears the cost of conservation, and who might pay for protection, restoration, monitoring, or data?
- Are claims of restoration, conservation impact, carbon benefit, or biodiversity gain independently measurable and durable?
- What permits, access permissions, community consent, benefit-sharing arrangements, and specialist qualifications are required?
- Can existing researchers, conservation organisations, laboratories, local stewards, and technical operators supply capabilities that a new entrant can support or coordinate responsibly?

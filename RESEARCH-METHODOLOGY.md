# Research Methodology

## Research record
For each investigation, record:
- Research question and why it matters
- Evidence required
- Sources and source quality
- Publication date and observation period
- Method, definitions, and assumptions
- Findings and alternative explanations
- Limitations and uncertainty
- Conclusion status
- Next action

## Conclusion labels
- **Established fact:** directly supported by reliable evidence for the stated scope.
- **Supported interpretation:** evidence supports this explanation, but it is not uniquely proven.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible explanation that needs testing.
- **Unresolved:** evidence is insufficient or conflicting.
- **Contradicted:** reliable evidence conflicts with the claim.

## Data discipline
- Distinguish nominal values from inflation-adjusted values.
- Distinguish stocks measured at a point in time from flows measured over a period.
- Record units, currency, seasonal adjustment, frequency, and observation dates.
- Distinguish the publication date from the period the data describe.
- Record revisions and methodology changes where known.
- Mark missing or unreliable data; do not silently fill gaps.
- Avoid cross-country comparisons unless definitions and coverage are sufficiently comparable.

## Causal discipline
A plausible mechanism is not proof of causation. Consider timing, alternative explanations, confounders, policy changes, and strength of evidence.

## State-classification discipline
Do not classify an economic state from one indicator alone. Do not invent thresholds. Develop candidate rules only after definitions and historical behavior have been examined. Record confirmation and invalidation evidence.

## Change control
When a definition or conclusion changes, record the previous version, new version, reason, motivating evidence, and implications for earlier analyses.

# Footprint Intelligence Architecture

**Status:** Workstream D is formally designated in the project's workstream framework. The capabilities described here remain a proposed conceptual direction, not an implemented or validated system.

## Purpose and scope

Footprint Intelligence is a proposed capability for collecting and investigating observable traces across domains, identifying entities and relationships, and testing whether patterns reveal meaningful changes in larger systems. A **footprint** is an observable trace or measurement that may provide evidence about an entity, activity, condition, relationship, or change.

Footprints may be digital or non-digital. Examples include:

- Digital activity and public online information.
- Corporate announcements, hiring activity, product listings, and public business records.
- Financial disclosures, credit conditions, market prices, and capital flows.
- Government policies, public procurement, budgets, and institutional decisions.
- Satellite imagery, land-use changes, transport movements, and infrastructure development.
- Shipping activity, trade flows, port congestion, and supply-chain disruptions.
- Environmental measurements, weather, pollution, and water availability.
- Biological observations, health surveillance, agricultural conditions, and ecological changes.
- Scientific publications, patents, clinical trials, technical releases, and research investment.
- Demographic, behavioral, and population-level measurements, where appropriately sourced and used.

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive. Add footprint categories only when they provide genuine analytical value. Evidence that appears unrelated may justify investigation, but co-occurrence alone does not establish a relationship.

## Role as Workstream D

Footprint Intelligence is a reusable investigation capability that may serve the Global Economic State Transition Model and other domain-intelligence projects. It is distinct from Workstream C: C monitors economic developments and interprets evidence within the economic model; D investigates traces and relationships across relevant domains, whether or not they immediately relate to an economic indicator. C may request a focused D investigation, and D may provide relevant findings, evidence, uncertainty, and limitations to C. C may also use evidence without a dedicated D investigation.

The capability, its biological-related extensions, and any future supporting infrastructure remain proposed. Workstream D's formal designation does not claim these activities are operational, comprehensive, or validated.

Footprint Intelligence as a capability, the domains in which it may be applied, and products or projects that may use its outputs are separate levels. Potential domains include economic, financial, industrial, supply-chain, geographic, environmental, biological, technological, scientific, demographic, and social systems. These are possible application areas, not new workstreams or products to establish automatically. Do not force every finding into the economic model; only relevant, sufficiently assessed evidence should be transferred to Workstream C or another appropriate project.

Workstreams A, B, C, and D have distinct responsibilities and may inform one another through justified feedback. Their authoritative definitions and boundaries are in `RESEARCH-METHODOLOGY.md`.

## Two complementary investigation paths

### Top-down investigation

Begin with a question, system, industry, economic pressure, entity, or potential risk. Identify its components, dependencies, relationships, possible mechanisms, relevant footprints, and the evidence needed to investigate it.

### Bottom-up discovery

Begin with observations or signals from one or more sources. Identify relevant entities, examine possible relationships, investigate patterns or anomalies, and develop questions about potentially overlooked relationships, dependencies, risks, opportunities, or system changes.

In plain language, top-down work starts with a question and seeks evidence; bottom-up work starts with evidence and discovers which questions deserve investigation. Both paths feed into the same evidence-disciplined process. A detected pattern is a lead to investigate, not a conclusion by itself.

## Shared intelligence process

The general conceptual process is:

**Observable footprints → source and evidence assessment → entity identification → relationship and dependency analysis → pattern and anomaly investigation → mechanism and transmission analysis → verification and alternative explanations → implications, scenarios, and decision support.**

This is not a rigid or necessarily linear pipeline. Investigation may move back and forth as evidence changes; relationships may form feedback loops; and hypotheses may be revised or rejected. At each stage, distinguish:

- Direct observations from interpretations.
- Established facts from hypotheses.
- Correlation from demonstrated causation.
- A plausible mechanism from a verified mechanism.
- A general incentive from evidence of a specific actor's intentions.
- A detected pattern from a validated predictive signal.
- Confirming evidence from contradictory evidence.
- Known information from missing or uncertain information.

Where the future evidence model supports it, preserve source provenance, observation and publication dates, definitions, limitations, uncertainty, and confidence. Identify entities and relationships carefully: names, locations, similar timing, or shared characteristics can be ambiguous and are not by themselves proof of identity, coordination, or causation.

## Biological Intelligence

Biology is a foundational interacting domain in this proposed architecture, not merely a subcategory of healthcare or an isolated field. Its scope includes biological systems and their connections to human activity, money, economic incentives, technology, institutions, and power.

The following initial working taxonomy is not complete or fixed:

1. **Molecular and physiological intelligence:** genetics, proteins, metabolites, hormones, immune responses, microbiomes, physiological measurements, and biological responses to environmental conditions.
2. **Human behavior and cognitive systems:** sleep, stress, learning, cognition, behavioral patterns, and interactions between biological conditions and decision-making. A behavior or measurement does not, without sufficient evidence, reveal a person's intentions or psychological state.
3. **Infectious disease and microbial systems:** pathogen and genomic surveillance, antimicrobial resistance, wastewater monitoring, outbreak dynamics, and possible economic implications of disease transmission.
4. **Agricultural, livestock, and ecological systems:** crop health, livestock disease, soil biology, pests, pollinators, biodiversity, ecosystem changes, food production, and economic resilience.
5. **Biotechnology and biological capability:** pharmaceutical research, clinical trials, genetic technologies, biological manufacturing, patents, diagnostic systems, research capacity, and biotechnology investment.
6. **Population and demographic biology:** fertility, mortality, ageing, migration, nutrition, population health, workforce capacity, and possible implications for economic development, public finances, and capital allocation.

These are areas for potential investigation, not claims that the project currently collects or analyzes such data.

## Bioeconomic Intelligence

**Bioeconomic Intelligence** is a proposed cross-domain capability for investigating how biological conditions and processes interact with money, capital, production, labor, consumption, institutions, and markets. Potential lines of inquiry include:

- Health and nutrition with labor productivity.
- Disease with healthcare expenditure, insurance, and public finances.
- Agricultural conditions with food supply, commodity prices, and inflation.
- Environmental exposure with health costs, regulation, and corporate liabilities.
- Fertility, ageing, and migration with labor supply, pensions, and economic growth.
- Biotechnology with patents, research funding, manufacturing, and investment.
- Biological data with ownership, consent, privacy, commercial value, and governance.
- Biological resources with industrial production, supply chains, trade, and geopolitical dependencies.

Where evidence permits, investigate each step in a possible transmission pathway. For example, a biological or ecological change might affect physical production, then supply and costs, then prices, company earnings, credit exposure, government expenditure, and financial markets. Evidence for one link does not establish the entire chain: investigate and support each material link separately. Consider reverse transmission and feedback too; economic conditions may affect nutrition, healthcare access, environmental exposure, population health, and biological outcomes.

## Biological Power and Governance Intelligence

**Biological Power and Governance Intelligence** is a related proposed capability for investigating the ownership, control, funding, use, and governance of biological capabilities and biological data. Potential topics include:

- Pharmaceutical and biotechnology ownership; research funding and commercial incentives.
- Patents, licensing, and intellectual property.
- Biological manufacturing and strategic supply chains.
- Ownership, access, and privacy of genetic and health data.
- Public-health policy and institutional authority.
- Conflicts of interest, lobbying, regulatory capture, and documented misconduct.
- Environmental health risks and the distribution of economic costs.
- Dual-use biological research and legitimate biosecurity concerns.
- Historical or contemporary allegations of concealed activities, coercion, or deliberate harm.

Controversial allegations merit disciplined investigation; controversy alone is not grounds to dismiss a claim. But possibility, an actor's incentive, or past misconduct does not establish that a specific claim is true. For a material claim:

1. State the exact claim and its scope.
2. Identify the proposed mechanism and the actors or relationships alleged.
3. Examine what evidence supports those actors, relationships, ownership, funding, contracts, incentives, authority, or possible benefits.
4. Identify evidence that could support or contradict the claim.
5. Assess source reliability and independence, and consider alternative explanations.
6. Record uncertainty, confidence, missing evidence, and what could change the assessment.

Keep established historical facts, current verified facts, plausible but unverified hypotheses, unsupported allegations, and claims contradicted by strong evidence distinct. Do not infer intent from incentives alone.

## Relationship to broader System Intelligence

Footprint Intelligence and its domain capabilities may support investigation across interacting:

- Economic and financial systems.
- Industrial and supply-chain systems.
- Government and institutional systems.
- Geographic and physical systems.
- Environmental and ecological systems.
- Biological and population systems.
- Technology, digital data, and scientific systems.
- Human behavior and social systems.

These are conceptual domains and analytical capabilities, not a prescription to create separate software products. They may share evidence, entity, relationship, mechanism, and verification concepts. The architecture should allow both investigation of predefined systems and discovery of previously unrecognized relationships, with gradual, evidence-led development rather than assumed immediate integration.

## Relationship to the Global Economic State Transition Model

This proposed broader intelligence direction does not replace or automatically expand the existing economic measurement framework. The model retains its six foundational indicator candidates:

1. Real GDP growth.
2. Labor productivity growth.
3. Private-sector credit growth.
4. Private-sector credit relative to GDP.
5. Debt-service burden.
6. Productive investment.

Its agreed four layers remain: core economic indicators; supporting economic measurements; economic mechanisms and transmission; and evidence-based economic state assessment. Footprint and biological-related evidence may eventually help interpret supporting measurements or investigate mechanisms and emerging pressures. Such signals do not automatically become core indicators. Before they influence formal conclusions, establish their definitions and reliability and investigate the relevant economic transmission. Do not change the existing immediate task: complete the measurement specification for Indicator 1, real GDP growth, in `03-measurement/01-real-gdp-growth.md`. Do not interrupt that workflow or begin formal state classification.

## Evidence, ethics, and safeguards

Any future work under this architecture should:

- Assess source quality, provenance, scope, timeliness, independence, and limitations.
- Seek confirming and contradictory evidence and consider credible alternative explanations.
- Separate observation, inference, hypothesis, allegation, and conclusion; state uncertainty rather than filling evidentiary gaps with speculation.
- Treat anomalies and cross-source matches as leads requiring verification, not proof or validated prediction.
- Use data only through lawful access and respect applicable privacy, consent, confidentiality, and research-ethics requirements.
- Apply heightened care to identifiable people and sensitive biological, health, behavioral, or genetic information; avoid unnecessary collection, exposure, or inference about individuals.
- Record missing evidence and limits on what can responsibly be concluded.

Ethical and legal review is part of the proposed direction, not a substitute for establishing evidence or for applicable expert review.

## Capability status and boundaries

- **Established project work:** The economic model has six foundational indicator candidates, an agreed four-layer framework, and an active measurement-specification workflow. These are documented project decisions, not evidence that a broader intelligence platform exists.
- **Proposed capabilities:** Footprint Intelligence, Biological Intelligence, Bioeconomic Intelligence, Biological Power and Governance Intelligence, and their cross-domain connections are conceptual directions only.
- **Hypotheses and allegations:** Specific relationships, mechanisms, predictions, and claims remain hypotheses or allegations until investigated against appropriate evidence. Controversy neither validates nor invalidates them.
- **Future work:** Define feasible and ethical research questions, evidence requirements, and priorities incrementally. No data-collection system, surveillance capability, automated inference, software, or biological-data processing is specified or implemented by this document.

This architecture is intended to guide careful future inquiry while preserving the existing economic measurement work and its evidence standards.

# Historical Event and Process Registry

**Status:** Initial registry structure; no event has been independently investigated or validated by creating this file.  
**Purpose:** Discover, index and connect historical events and longer-running processes whose consequences may still shape present conditions or future developments.

## 1. Registry rules

- This is an open-ended discovery inventory, not a complete history, fixed syllabus, importance ranking or whitelist.
- Include events and processes across all geographies, domains, populations and time horizons.
- Include local, overlooked and underrepresented events where relevant evidence can be found.
- Register a candidate when it is worth tracking; registration does not mean its significance, consequences or causal effects are established.
- Link to substantive investigation records rather than placing an entire investigation in this index.
- Reuse existing Lifeconomist research and source records. Do not duplicate material merely to populate the registry.
- Distinguish a discrete event from a long-running process. Link them when evidence supports a relationship.
- Record uncertainty, contested accounts, missing evidence and competing explanations.
- Do not rank candidates or assign impact scores without a transparent, justified method.
- Update records when material new evidence changes the interpretation, consequence assessment or status.

## 2. Suggested record fields

Use only fields relevant to the candidate; this is a flexible schema, not a mandatory form.

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Record ID | Stable identifier, e.g. HE-0001 for an event or HP-0001 for a process |
| Name | Common name and useful alternative names |
| Record type | Discrete event, long-running process, structural transition, or linked cluster |
| Time period | Start/end dates or best-supported approximate period; state uncertainty |
| Geography | Locality, country, region or global scope |
| Domains | Economic, financial, political, social, technological, environmental, legal, health, agricultural, energy, demographic, etc. |
| Description | Brief neutral description of what occurred or changed |
| Context and baseline | Conditions and trends already present beforehand |
| Actors and institutions | Relevant parties, with roles distinguished and evidence linked |
| Candidate consequences | Initial possible immediate, indirect, delayed, cumulative or persistent effects |
| Structural changes | Candidate changes to institutions, rules, relationships, capabilities or material conditions |
| Transmission links | Other events, systems, places and populations potentially connected |
| Current relevance | Why the record may matter now |
| Future questions | Possible emerging effects, scenarios or indicators to monitor |
| Evidence status | Candidate, under investigation, supported, contested, superseded, or closed with rationale |
| Confidence and uncertainty | Explain confidence in specific claims; do not use a single label to hide mixed evidence |
| Sources | Primary and secondary evidence with provenance, dates and limitations |
| Investigation record | Link to detailed analysis when created |
| Related records | Linked events, processes, concepts, measurements, monitoring and footprint records |
| Last reviewed | Date and reason for material review |

## 3. Evidence status meanings

- **Candidate:** A lead worth considering; significance and effects have not yet been assessed.
- **Under investigation:** A substantive inquiry is underway; findings remain provisional.
- **Supported:** Specific claims or consequences have adequate evidence for the stated scope. This does not mean every interpretation is settled.
- **Contested:** Material accounts, evidence or interpretations conflict; record the disagreement.
- **Superseded:** A record or interpretation has been replaced by a better-scoped or more accurate record; preserve the link and reason.
- **Closed with rationale:** No further work is currently justified, or the inquiry reached a documented stopping point. This does not mean the event has no continuing effects.

Status applies to the record's investigation state, not to a simplistic judgement that an event itself is true or false. Individual claims may have different evidence status and confidence.

## 4. Initial domain discovery lenses

These are prompts for discovery, not a closed classification:

- Conflict, war, peace settlements and security arrangements.
- Political transitions, governance, state formation and institutional change.
- Laws, treaties, regulations, court decisions and public-policy shifts.
- Financial crises, monetary changes, debt events and changes in capital allocation.
- Trade, industrialisation, supply-chain restructuring and market integration.
- Scientific discoveries, inventions, technological diffusion and infrastructure.
- Pandemics, public-health changes and health-system transformations.
- Natural disasters, environmental shocks, ecological change and climate-related processes.
- Demographic shifts, migration, urbanisation and population change.
- Social movements, labour relations, cultural change and changes in social organisation.
- Energy, food, water, land and other resource-system transformations.
- Corporate, organisational and business-model changes with wider consequences.
- Gradual transitions and cumulative processes that may not have a single triggering event.
- Local and community developments whose effects may extend beyond their immediate location.

## 5. Suggested detailed investigation record

When a candidate warrants substantive work, create a separate record using a clear name such as `historical-events/HE-0001-short-name.md`. A useful outline is:

1. Research question and reason for investigation.
2. Event/process definition, dates, geography and scope.
3. What happened: chronology, actors, decisions and primary evidence.
4. Context and baseline: prior conditions, trends and vulnerabilities.
5. Immediate consequences.
6. Secondary and indirect consequences.
7. Delayed, cumulative and persistent consequences.
8. Structural changes and evidence of persistence.
9. Transmission pathways and interactions with other events/processes.
10. Distributional effects across places and populations.
11. Effects still visible today and what may be changing.
12. Future scenarios, assumptions and indicators.
13. Risks, unmet needs, opportunities and decision relevance.
14. Causal assessment, alternative explanations and counterfactual limits.
15. Evidence table, source quality, disagreements and uncertainty.
16. Conclusions, unresolved questions, related records and next useful action.

Do not create an empty detailed file for every registry candidate. Create one when there is a real investigation to preserve.

## 6. Initial registry

No event or process has been pre-populated as a verified finding. Candidates should be added as investigations are selected and evidence is assessed. A candidate list may be maintained here when useful, with each entry clearly labelled as uninvestigated until its evidence has been examined.
